# Area Brief — Check-ins & 1:1s — 2026-07-23

**Author:** Michelle
**Area:** Check-ins & 1:1s (VP2)
**Time-box:** 2-week scoping pass, updated 2026-07-23 with prototype-sourced additions. This is an honest map, not a finished spec.

> **One object, two names.** We use "check-in" and "1:1" for the **same thing**: a recurring one-on-one meeting between two people. It is the **meeting** (in person or video), with optional pre-work the employee fills in ahead of time ("how was your week"). It is **not** a stand-alone weekly survey. Saying this plainly up front stops anyone — a person or an AI agent — from building the wrong shape (a questionnaire nobody asked for) just because the brief uses both words.

> **Who the meeting is between matters.** The obvious case is a manager and one of their direct reports. But in practice, an employee might also have regular 1:1s with: an indirect manager (someone above their direct manager who checks in with them), a functional lead (a project owner who isn't their official manager), or a peer. The "manager" in the org chart is not always the only person having these conversations. Our model needs to accommodate this reality, not only the tidy chart case.

## One-line job
Help two people — whatever their working relationship — have a regular, useful conversation: agree what to talk about, keep notes, track what they decided to do, and carry the useful parts forward to the next talk and to the formal review.

## How to read the tags
Source: **[client]** interview-evidenced · **[sales]** raised by sales · **[market]** competitive-scan table stakes · **[team]** internal build decision · **[expert]** needs sign-off from a security or privacy expert · **[segment]** strength depends on client size.

*Note on evidence honesty:* the check-in interest in our interviews came mostly as **reaction to a screen**, not as a described current pain. Managers said "that would be useful" when shown the idea; few described a broken check-in workflow they run today. The one real, current behaviour is managers keeping their own private meeting notes (the District Manager's handwritten agenda notes and emailed reminders; the Operations Director wanting to replace his Word doc). So the note-taking core is **[client]**; much of the rest is **[market]** — a candidate we like, not a confirmed need. Tags below reflect that.

*Note on adoption:* managers are already doing the note-taking job — just outside the system. Word docs and notebooks are the competition, not the absence of a habit. Getting managers to write notes in Payworks instead requires the experience to be faster and more obviously useful than what they already have. The payoff that tips the balance is the review: if notes written in Payworks appear organised and ready at review time — and a manager who used Word has to start from scratch — the system wins at the moment it matters most. **This means the note-taking UX and the review-prep view are not independent features; they are the same adoption argument.** If either is clunky, managers stay in Word and the whole downstream value (AI surfacing, review carry-forward, history) never gets fed. the Operations Director named the design constraint plainly: "if it becomes complicated or cumbersome, it will fall apart."

## Must-haves (with source tags)
The vital few this area cannot ship without. Kept short on purpose.

### The meeting itself
- [ ] **A recurring 1:1 meeting object between any two people** — not limited to the manager/direct-report pair. On a set beat (daily / weekly / every two weeks / monthly). Distinct from a review; not folded into one. The pair is set when the 1:1 series is created and the relationship type is labelled (see next item). **[market] [team]**
- [ ] **A person can also create a single, one-off 1:1 (an ad-hoc meeting)** *(added 2026-07-23)* — a one-time meeting with another employee, with no cadence and no repeat. It has the same agenda, notes, and action-item capabilities as a recurring 1:1, but it does not create a series. This covers the case where someone needs a one-time conversation that still gets a shared agenda and notes (not just a calendar invite with no record). **[team]**
- [ ] **A labelled relationship on every 1:1** — when a 1:1 series is created, the system records what the two people are to each other: **direct manager / direct report, indirect manager / indirect report, or peer**. This label drives who can see what (visibility rules), how the 1:1 surfaces at review time, and how it shows up in each person's list. A 1:1 with your direct manager and a 1:1 with a peer should look alike to use, but the system needs to know they're different. **Default when the relationship is unknown: peer** *(decided 2026-07-22)* — if the system can't tell what the two people are to each other (for example, it can't confirm an indirect-manager / indirect-report link from the org chart), it labels the 1:1 **peer**. Peer is the safest fallback because it carries the tightest visibility (only the two participants, no chain, no HR admin) — so an unknown relationship can never accidentally open the 1:1 wider than it should be. **[team] [expert]**
- [ ] **Either person can start a 1:1 with any other employee in the company** *(decided 2026-07-22)* — creation is not limited to the org chart. Any employee can open an indirect-manager or peer 1:1 series with any other employee. **Flag for the security expert:** this means starting a 1:1 is **not** gated by the normal access rules (security level, department, pay group) the way most actions are. The guard that keeps this safe is not *who you can start a 1:1 with* — it's *who can see the notes* (see the two note tiers below). The expert needs to confirm this split is acceptable: open creation, tightly-scoped visibility. **[team] [expert]**
- [ ] **A shared agenda both people can add to** — a list of what to talk about that either person can write to before and during the meeting. **[market]**
- [ ] **Admin-managed templates** — an administrator (Site Admin or HR Admin) can create and publish reusable agenda templates. Employees and managers pick from the list rather than starting from a blank page. Templates are for **consistency across the company**, not just convenience for one pair. Individual pairs can still customise their agenda; the template is a starting point, not a locked form. **One shared bank for all relationship types** *(decided 2026-07-22)* — there is a single template bank; we are **not** building separate banks for manager, indirect, and peer 1:1s. **[market] [team]**
- [ ] **Templates and pre-work content are bilingual (EN/FR)** *(added 2026-07-22)* — admin-authored content that ships across the company must carry both English and French, the same obligation the Reviews question bank already honours. This applies to agenda templates now, and to pre-work questions if/when pre-work is built. A developer building the template editor needs to know each template holds an EN and an FR version, not one language. **[market] [team]**
- [ ] **Carry-forward of open items, with a resolution path** *(clarified 2026-07-23)* — an agenda item has one of three states: **open** (still to discuss), **resolved** (done — ticked off), or **dropped** (deliberately let go). Only **open** items carry forward to the next meeting. Resolved and dropped items stay in the record but do not carry. This prevents the agenda from becoming an unusable backlog of items that were never cleared (see risk #8). **[market] [team]**
- [ ] **Action items with an owner** — a decided next step, tagged to who owns it, that persists and carries forward. **[market]**

### Notes and who can see them
- [ ] **1:1 notes are the same note object as the Feedback area's note, written from the 1:1** *(decided 2026-07-22, PM + Margaret + Claire)* — check-in notes do **not** live in a separate Check-ins store. They write into the **shared note object owned by the Feedback & Performance Notes brief**, tagged to this 1:1 series. One note surface across the two areas, not two. What differs is **visibility**: a 1:1 note carries the participant-pair scope below. A developer building this reads and writes the Feedback note object; it does not define its own. See the cross-area note (INT-12) and `feedback-and-performance-notes-area-brief-2026-07-22.md` (D6-NOTE). **[team] [expert]**
- [ ] **Two note tiers, as two clearly separate sections** *(clarified 2026-07-22)* — the conversation has **one section for shared notes and one section for private notes**, and it must be unmistakable on screen which is which. There is **no single "new note" that defaults to a tier** — the person writes into the section they mean. (1) **Private notes** are visible only to the person who wrote them (author-only; the other person and admins cannot see them). (2) **Shared notes** are visible to the people who have access to the conversation (see the visibility rule below). **[client] [team] [expert]**
- [ ] **Shared notes are visible to only the two participants** *(decided 2026-07-22)* — for **every** relationship type, a shared note is seen by the two people in that 1:1 and no one else. This is the deliberate answer to "who else can see a 1:1": for a peer 1:1, nobody's manager and no HR admin; for a manager 1:1, the same — just the pair. **Flag for the security expert:** this is a per-record, two-named-people grant. It does **not** ride the manager-chain RBS model, which is built to let people *up the chain* see down. The expert must confirm the access model can enforce a participant-pair scope, or tell us what new mechanism is needed. Do not let a developer fall back to "visible up the chain" because that is what RBS does by default — that would leak the conversation. **[client] [team] [expert]**
- [ ] **The two sections make the tier obvious** — because private and shared live in separate, clearly labelled sections, no one can mistake a private note for a shared one, or the reverse. A wrong guess here is a privacy leak, so the separation must be unmistakable. **[team] [expert]**
- [ ] **OPEN — one note object, two visibility regimes** *(new 2026-07-22, from the note-object fold)* — now that a 1:1 shared note and Feedback are the **same object** (top of this section), that one object must support **two different visibility regimes on the same store**: the **strict participant-pair scope** here (only the two people, never a chain, never HR) **and** the Feedback side's **still-open read-scope** (who reads feedback: target manager, a new manager, HR/admins — undecided; see the Feedback brief's read-scope cluster). These cannot be settled independently on one object. The expert must confirm a single note object can enforce **both** — a per-record participant-pair grant for 1:1 shared notes and the more open feedback read-scope — **without one leaking into the other** (a 1:1 shared note must never become HR-readable just because the object also holds HR-readable feedback). This is the access/visibility question the fold introduced; it is **not** the same as the enforceability flag below (which is only about the pair-scope in isolation). **[$] [expert]**
- [ ] **Shared notes can be edited; no conversions between tiers** *(decided 2026-07-22)* — a shared note can be edited after it is written. A note **cannot be moved from private to shared or from shared to private** — the two sections do not swap content. This removes a whole class of "I made my private note public by accident" risk. Edits to a shared note should be attributed through the app's existing audit attribution (see Compliance below). **[team] [expert]**

### Staying on schedule
- [ ] **A cadence and a next-meeting date** — the object knows when the next 1:1 is due. **[market]**
- [ ] **Reminders shortly before the meeting** — a nudge so the meeting isn't forgotten. The **light version is an email reminder before the event**; full calendar sync (Google / Outlook) is a heavier build and sits in open questions, not here. **[market] [client]**

### Scheduling — creating and changing a 1:1
- [ ] **A series has a start date** *(added 2026-07-23)* — the person sets when the series begins. The first occurrence falls on this date, and future occurrences are calculated from it using the cadence. **[team]**
- [ ] **A series can be continuous or have an end date** *(added 2026-07-23)* — the person chooses one: continuous (runs until someone ends it) or ending on a specific date. If continuous, no end date is required. If an end date is chosen, it must fall on or after the start date. **[team]**
- [ ] **A person can change the cadence of an existing series** *(added 2026-07-23)* — for example, switching from weekly to every two weeks. The change applies to future occurrences only — past occurrences and their notes are never changed. **[team]**
- [ ] **A person can change the end date, or switch a series between having an end date and being continuous** *(added 2026-07-23)* — again, changes apply to future occurrences only. **[team]**
- [ ] **A person can reschedule a single occurrence** *(added 2026-07-23)* — move one meeting to a different date without changing the whole series. **[team]**
- [ ] **A person can skip or cancel a single occurrence** *(added 2026-07-23)* — for example, when one person is away. The cancelled occurrence keeps its place in the history (it happened, it was cancelled — it is not erased). When an occurrence is cancelled, its open agenda items carry forward to the next occurrence. **[team]**

**Open question for this section** *(new 2026-07-23)*: for monthly cadence, what does "the same day each month" mean when the day doesn't exist (e.g. the 31st in February)? And does biweekly count from the start date or from a fixed calendar week? A developer will guess these — worth pinning.

### Looking back — for the employee
- [ ] **An employee can easily review their own 1:1 history to prepare for their review** — an employee's view surfaces all their past 1:1 notes in one place, browsable and searchable. Because an employee may have 1:1s with more than one person (their direct manager, an indirect manager, a peer), **the view is filterable by relationship or by the other person** so they can pull what's relevant for a specific review context without wading through everything. **[client] [team]**

### Looking back — for the manager
- [ ] **A manager can easily review their 1:1 notes for a direct report before writing that report's review** — a manager's view for a given direct report shows all their 1:1 history with that person in one place, filterable and searchable. The goal is to walk into a review having read the record, not guessing from the last two weeks. **[client] [team]**
- [ ] **A searchable history of past 1:1s is available to both participants** — each person can look back at what was discussed and decided over time, scoped to the 1:1 series they are part of. **[market] [client]**
- [ ] **Each participant can see their upcoming 1:1s across all their series** *(added 2026-07-23)* — a combined view showing future scheduled meetings across every 1:1 the person is part of, alongside their past meeting history. This is not a second history screen — it extends the existing history view to include the future, so a person can see where they've been and what's coming in one place. The upcoming view inherits the same participant-pair visibility rule as notes — a person never sees a 1:1 they aren't in. **[team]**

### Link to goals
- [ ] **The 1:1 shows the employee's own goals** *(clarified 2026-07-22)* — the employee's active goals appear alongside the agenda so the meeting can talk about progress. **This reads the employee's own goals only** — not cascaded or team goals. This is **display only** as a must-have (the 1:1 just reads goals). Whether the 1:1 also **updates** goal progress is a fork — see the Check-ins → Goals interaction below. **Open dependency:** "active goals" still needs a shared definition with the Goals slice — see open questions. **[market] [client]**

Check-ins touches three other areas. These are the seams where two briefs must agree. Listed here on purpose so that when the six briefs are stacked, nobody discovers an overlap late.

- **Check-ins → Reviews (history as supporting documentation).** Past 1:1 notes should be available at review time — especially useful when the person **running the review is not the manager who wrote the notes** (a new manager, or a skip-level reviewer). For the employee, the filterable 1:1 history view is what they use to prepare. For the manager, it's their history view for that direct report. **Decided 2026-07-22:** a **skip-level or new reviewer does NOT see shared notes from a 1:1 they were not part of** — and there is **no "bring forward" mechanism** that would let anyone widen that. This holds the "shared = only the two participants" rule (above) all the way into Reviews. In the review workspace, the read-only context panel shows a reviewer the 1:1 notes **only for 1:1s they were personally a participant in** (their own direct-report 1:1s); it shows nothing from 1:1s they weren't in. If an employee wants a new reviewer to know something from an old 1:1, the employee writes it into their **self-assessment** themselves — ordinary review content, no special mechanism, no visibility rule broken. **Build requirement this creates:** the panel must tag each note's origin (record-level vs. which 1:1 series) and filter 1:1-originated notes by reviewer participation — see the cross-area note (INT-11). **[client] [team]**
- **Check-ins → Goals (display vs. write-back).** The 1:1 shows the employee's own goals (must-have, display only). **Open fork:** does the 1:1 also **write** goal progress inline, or only display them? Inline write-back takes a hard dependency on the Goals object being stable — and Goals is being scoped in parallel right now. Display-only is the low-dependency safe build. **Product answer (2026-07-21): Display only for VP1.** **Still open (2026-07-22):** what counts as an **"active goal"** the 1:1 reads — this needs one shared definition agreed with the Goals slice (their status model is on-track / off-track / done). Confirmed the 1:1 reads the **employee's own** goals only, not cascaded or team goals. Do not commit to write-back until Goals is ready. **Peer 1:1s do not show a goals rail** *(decided 2026-07-23)* — in a peer 1:1, there is no single "employee" whose goals to display (both people are peers). The goals view does not appear. **Open question:** should **indirect-manager 1:1s** (skip-level) also show the employee's goals? The must-have (above) says "the employee's own goals appear alongside the agenda so the meeting can talk about progress" — but that was written with the direct-manager case in mind. A skip-level conversation may not be about goal progress; showing goals there could set the wrong expectation or feel like surveillance from above. Decide whether the goals rail appears in direct-manager 1:1s only, or in both direct and indirect. **[market] [team] [segment]**
- **Check-ins → Role Profiles / Reports-To (who is paired with whom).** A manager/direct-report 1:1 assumes a pairing pulled from the org chart, which inherits the **same broken-Reports-To failure class Reviews spent weeks on**: no manager, terminated manager, employee with a missing Reports-To. **Note (2026-07-22):** Role Profiles has flagged an unresolved **blocker** — how an employee gets one primary role and a valid manager link — and our manager/direct-report 1:1 pairing rides on that. This is not resolved just because Reviews touched it; it stays live on our register. Peer and indirect-manager 1:1s are user-created and don't depend on the org chart — but they introduce their own questions (who can create them: **either person, decided 2026-07-22**; who can end them: still open). We do not re-solve Reports-To here; we point at it. See risks. **[team] [expert]**
- **Check-ins → Dashboards (check-in status as a tile).** *(added 2026-07-22)* The Performance Dashboards note surfaces check-in signals on **both** surfaces — the manager dashboard shows "1:1 overdue" (with the employee name) and a check-ins-overdue roll-up; the HR dashboard shows "check-in coverage" and which managers have teams behind on check-ins. **Check-ins owns these metrics; Dashboards displays them** (one source, shown in the dashboard — the dashboard produces no check-in data of its own). The dependency runs the other way for **one definition**: a dashboard tile can't call a 1:1 "overdue" without a threshold — *how long past cadence counts as overdue* — and that line is **ours to set, not the dashboard's.** We have a cadence and a next-meeting date but have **not** defined the overdue threshold; see open questions. Overdue nudges ride the shared notification system (same as our meeting reminders). See cross-area note INT-1 (check-in coverage instance) and INT-2. **[team] [segment]**

## Risks / break points
Every place this could go wrong that we can find, solved or not. Expensive ones marked **[$]** — a wrong guess costs privacy, compliance, or an action you can't take back.

- **A private note leaks.** If the screen ever shows a private (author-only) note to the other person or to an admin, that's a direct privacy breach and it kills trust in the whole feature. The two-section layout (separate private and shared sections) is the guard; the separation must be unmistakable. **[$]**
- **Shared notes get scoped wider than the two participants.** *(sharpened 2026-07-22)* The decided rule is: shared notes are visible to **only the two people in the 1:1**, for every relationship type. The risk in build is a developer falling back to the manager-chain RBS default — which is built to let people *up the chain* see down — and thereby exposing a peer or indirect 1:1 to a manager or HR admin. The participant-pair scope is a per-record grant that the chain-based RBS model does not natively provide. Needs sign-off from the access/security expert that the model can enforce a two-named-people scope before design finalises the permission model. **[$] [expert]**
- **Open creation, but scoped visibility — confirm the pair is safe together.** *(new 2026-07-22)* Anyone can start a 1:1 with anyone (open creation, decided). Safety rests entirely on the visibility rule (only the two participants see shared notes). If visibility is ever loosened, open creation becomes a way to fish for information. Guard: open creation and participant-only visibility must ship together, and the expert must confirm the combination. **[$] [expert]**
- **Broken Reports-To.** No manager, a terminated manager, or an employee reporting to more than one manager — the manager/direct-report 1:1 has no valid pair. Same failure class as Reviews, and it rides Role Profiles' unresolved primary-role blocker; unattended, a 1:1 could attach to nobody or the wrong manager. **[$]**
- **Manager changes mid-series.** *(decided 2026-07-22)* When an employee gets a new manager, the **new manager starts fresh** — a new 1:1 series, not an inheritance of the old one. This means the old manager's shared and private notes stay with the old (now-inactive) series and are **not** handed to the new manager. Remaining build questions: does the old series become read-only/archived, and does the employee retain access to their own history across both series for review prep? Define the archived-series state. **[$]**
- **Employee leaves mid-series.** What happens to the 1:1 record, the open items, and the shared notes when someone is terminated? Scope this **against the app's existing terminated-employee rules** (auto-suspend of linked accounts; ESS "access for terminated employees" settings) rather than inventing a new rule. **Stated constraint (2026-07-22):** whatever happens to the recurring meeting, the **remaining participant retains access to the notes** — termination of one person must not strip the other's access to the shared and their own private notes from that series. What happens to the **recurrence itself** (does the series end, pause, or go read-only when one participant is terminated) is **open**. **[$]**
- **Multiple 1:1 series, one employee, cross-leaking notes.** An employee with 1:1s with both their direct manager and an indirect manager now has two series. The system must ensure a **private** note written in one series is invisible in the other — even if the same manager is in both. **And (2026-07-22):** confirm the **shared**-note case too — a shared note lives only within its own series and its two participants; if the same manager is in two series with the same employee, shared notes are still series-isolated, not pooled. The relationship label is not enough; note-scope must be pinned to the individual series. **[$]**
- **Review prep pulls the wrong 1:1s.** If an employee's review prep view surfaces peer 1:1 notes alongside manager 1:1 notes without clear labelling, an employee might submit peer-conversation notes as review evidence when they shouldn't, or miss the notes that actually matter. The filter-by-relationship feature is the guard here; it must work correctly. Reinforced by the Reviews decision (2026-07-22): a reviewer sees 1:1 notes in the review panel **only for 1:1s they were a participant in**, and there is no bring-forward path that changes this. **[$]**
- **Goals write-back with no Goals object.** If we build inline goal updates before Goals is stable, the 1:1 writes to something that isn't finished. Display-only for VP1 avoids this. Guard the dependency; also pin down the "active goal" definition with Goals before even the read view ships. **[$]**
- **Pre-work with no meeting.** The employee fills in "how was your week," then the 1:1 never happens (cancelled, manager away). Where does that pre-work live, and can it get lost? Deferred with pre-work to NTH — flag that when pre-work returns, orphaned pre-work must not silently attach to a cancelled meeting's record. Undefined.
- **Carry-forward pile-up.** If open items always roll forward and are never cleared, the agenda becomes an unusable backlog. Needs a way to close or drop an item, not just carry it.
- **An action item outlives its context.** An action item tied to a goal or a person that later changes or leaves — does it dangle? Undefined.
- **Admin template scope creep.** If admins can publish templates that are mandatory or locked, the 1:1 stops feeling like a conversation and starts feeling like a compliance form. The brief assumes templates are starting points, not enforced scripts — but that boundary has to be designed deliberately. **[team]**
- **Cancelled meeting loses its agenda.** *(new 2026-07-23)* When a single occurrence is cancelled (skipped), its open agenda items must carry forward to the next occurrence — not drop silently. If they drop, the person who prepped for the meeting loses their work and has to remember to re-add everything. This is the same seam as the "pre-work with no meeting" risk, just on the agenda side: a cancelled meeting should not eat its content. **[$]**
- **Bilingual gap in admin content.** *(new 2026-07-22)* Admin-published templates (and later, pre-work questions) ship company-wide and must carry EN and FR. If the template editor is built single-language, French clients get English-only agendas — a compliance and usability miss on a Canadian platform, and one the Reviews slice already guards against. **[$]**
- **Managers never leave Word.** The note-taking behaviour we want to move into Payworks already exists — in notebooks, Word docs, and emails. If the in-system experience is slower or less convenient than what managers already use, adoption fails quietly: the feature ships, but the notes stay in Word, and nothing downstream (review carry-forward, history, AI surfacing) ever gets fed. There is no forcing function. The only lever is making the in-system experience obviously worth switching. This is a product design risk, not an edge case. **[$]**

## Competitive notes
*Full scan is its own artifact (`area-brief-checkins-competitive-2026-07-17`); this is the "so what."*
Same vendor set as Reviews: Lattice, 15Five, Leapsome (mid-market); Rise, Humi, Collage, Knit, Wagepoint (Canadian SMB); Dayforce (enterprise reference).

- **The split is real and it's our opening.** The mid-market (Lattice / 15Five / Leapsome) treats the 1:1 as a first-class recurring object — shared agenda, two note tiers, carry-forward, goals link. The Canadian SMB set mostly does **not**: Humi, Knit, and Wagepoint have **no 1:1 / check-in object at all.** Only Rise and Collage have "check-ins," and theirs are lighter, questionnaire-style objects that feed a 1:1 held elsewhere — not the collaborative live-agenda meeting.
- **Table stakes** (what a credible feature needs): a distinct recurring 1:1 with a shared agenda + admin-managed templates; two note tiers (private + shared); action items and carry-forward; reminders; searchable history; a link to goals; and notes that feed the review. These map to our must-haves above.
- **On templates specifically.** Leapsome has the most configurable template model — admin-defined sections and talking-point banks, per-item privacy toggle. 15Five and Lattice both offer reusable templates. None of the Canadian SMB tools with check-in objects (Rise, Collage) surface admin-managed templates prominently. This is a real differentiator in our market.
- **On non-manager 1:1s.** 15Five explicitly supports peer 1:1s alongside manager/direct-report 1:1s. Lattice's model is primarily manager-chain. Leapsome allows any two participants. For our target segment (20–500 EE), peer 1:1s are less commonly a structured, systemised behaviour at the smaller end — but indirect-manager 1:1s may be: flat organisations where a founder or senior leader checks in with people below their direct reports is a real pattern across this range.
- **Conscious enterprise over-build to skip / defer** (revisit only beyond ~500 EE): sentiment / pulse analytics, org-wide "is everyone doing their 1:1s" compliance dashboards, deep 360 / competency / calibration, global-viewer role matrices, OKR alignment hierarchies. Give a **light** goals link, not a cascade.
- **On the note tiers.** All three mid-market tools support author-only private notes inside the 1:1 (that tier is universal). What they deliberately keep **out** of the collaborative 1:1 is the *employee-invisible-but-shared-with-others* note. They push that into the review / feedback / record layer, to protect the 1:1 as a trust space. Our decision (2026-07-22) goes further and cleaner: shared = only the two participants, no third-party visibility at all, and no conversion between private and shared. This keeps the 1:1 feeling like a safe conversation — the right call.

## Handoff note to dev
The core is a **recurring 1:1 between any two people** (plus one-off ad-hoc meetings), labelled with their relationship (direct manager / indirect manager / peer), with a **shared agenda, one shared admin-managed template bank (bilingual EN/FR), carry-forward with a three-state resolution path (open / resolved / dropped), action items, two note tiers held in two clearly separate sections (private + shared), reminders, and a display-only view of the employee's own goals (direct-manager 1:1s; no goals rail for peer 1:1s).** Either person can start a 1:1 with any other employee. A series has a start date, a cadence (daily / weekly / every two weeks / monthly), and is either continuous or has an end date; individual occurrences can be rescheduled, skipped, or cancelled. Both participants also get **filtered history views** — the employee to prepare for their own review, the manager to prepare for a direct report's review — **plus a combined upcoming-meetings view across all their series.**

The **one thing not to guess on** is note visibility, in three dimensions: (1) **private vs. shared** — held in two separate sections, no conversion between them, ever; (2) **shared notes are visible to only the two participants** — never up a manager chain, never to HR admins, for any relationship type; and (3) whether notes from one series (e.g. a peer 1:1) are visible in another (they must not be, private or shared). A wrong default — especially anything that silently exposes a private note, widens shared notes beyond the pair, or crosses a series boundary — is a privacy exposure, not a bug. This participant-pair scope does **not** ride the chain-based RBS model and needs the security expert to confirm the model can enforce it. **And the fold (decided 2026-07-22):** these 1:1 notes are **not** a Check-ins-local store — they are the **same note object as the Feedback area's** (written from the 1:1). That object now holds both record-level notes and 1:1-originated notes, so it must carry **two visibility regimes at once** — the strict participant-pair scope here, and the Feedback side's still-open read-scope — without one leaking into the other. A developer builds against the Feedback note object, not a new one; see the note-model section above and cross-area INT-12. Also do not wire **goal write-back** or **calendar sync** without a decision from us, and do not build the goals read view until the **"active goal" definition** is agreed with the Goals slice: these take dependencies (Goals being ready; a real integration) that are not settled.
