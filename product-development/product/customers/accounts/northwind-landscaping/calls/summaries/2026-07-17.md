## Executive Summary

This was the first working session with Northwind's HR leadership on the Performance Management module, and it opened with an unusually complete origin story: both HR leaders walked us through how their review process got to where it is, why it lives in QuickBase, and what would have to be true for Payworks to displace it. Their Head of HR had a hard stop at the 36-minute mark and used her closing minutes to commit the account to the pilot — *"we want to be part of your pilot to make it right." — Their Head of HR* — before handing the session to their HR Manager, whom she named as the owner: *"the HR Manager is definitely gonna be the builder for this, and gonna be the SME." — Their Head of HR*. Their HR Manager then spent the second half taking us through the current process in detail and driving the prototype herself.

This call focused on four areas: (1) the history of their review process and why it sits in QuickBase, (2) what consolidation into Payworks would actually have to do to displace it, (3) the structure of the review itself — scope, cadence, scoring, weighting and the goal cascade, and (4) first reactions to the prototype, which they navigated for roughly 30 minutes.

**How the process got here.** Their Head of HR framed it as a four-stage evolution driven by growth: *"our journey for Northwind Landscaping started as a very small company." — Their Head of HR*, and *"when you have small companies and small populations, you start with manual processes, which is totally fine." — Their Head of HR*. Their HR Manager built the first version herself. Their HR Manager gave the fullest version of the arc: *"And before that, it was just all paper-based, and then before that, we didn't even have anything. They weren't doing anything, so… we went from paper-based to hand-bombing everything into an Excel spreadsheet." — Their HR Manager*, landing at *"our QuickBase system that we have now, that captures and keeps the trending of the performance of a person." — Their HR Manager*. The move to QuickBase happened roughly two to three years ago, built in-house with their IT and business-development teams, when the spreadsheet stopped scaling: *"we need to get a little bit more automated than from an Excel spreadsheet that was, you know, highly manual for people. It was hard to track, it was hard to, like, manage as we continued to grow." — Their Head of HR*. Note what QuickBase is and is not, in their own words: *"it's not an LMS, it's not anything like that" — Their Head of HR* — it is a general in-house platform their IT team configured, not a bought HR product. **Nobody chose QuickBase over Payworks. They chose it over a spreadsheet, because Payworks had nothing to choose.**

**What consolidation would have to do.** The incumbent's failure is not its review UX — it is the seam on both sides of it. *"I have to do every single spreadsheet, every single thing manually, export it from PayWorks. Hopefully, all of the information is right. I have to scrub it manually. It's not linked to Cubase at all, QuickBase at all." — Their Head of HR* (their rendering of QuickBase), and on the far side, *"it still doesn't generate the report we need that we have to upload into PayWorks to change the rating of someone." — Their HR Manager*. Their stated minimum bar is worth recording precisely, because it is **lower than full consolidation**: *"It doesn't need to be within our pay system, meaning not within PayWorks, but if it could at least link" — Their Head of HR*. Consolidation is the preference — *"it would be really great to have it all in a one-stop shop." — Their Head of HR* — but an integration that removes the export/re-key/upload loop clears the bar she set on this call. Their Head of HR also named where the expectation comes from: *"I have a more progressive leader that has… has come from an ADP or an SAP background, same as me, where we're like, why can't this just be in our pay system, and you just grab everything?" — Their Head of HR*. Her description of the target state is a complete requirement in one sentence: *"it automatically dumps into a report, and it pulls from last year, gives you the analysis, and it'll attach to, you know, as soon as you put in the percentage increase" — Their Head of HR*.

**The cost of the seam, quantified in their own terms.** *"I've spent days, days, days, and days doing manual work to make sure that the data is accurate" — Their Head of HR*. Their HR Manager on the compensation half: *"if there's any compensation bonus increases that have to go through from the ratings, and looking at what was the increase last year, when were they hired, and all this kind of stuff, it's… It's just a nightmare, as you can imagine." — Their HR Manager*. And when Margaret named the reviews-to-payroll handoff as the pain point, the answer was one word long: *"Ugh, it's horrible." — Their HR Manager*. That thread was explicitly parked for the next session — it is the single largest unmapped area after this call.

**Review history in the employee record.** Their HR Manager raised this unprompted in her opening as the thing she expects from a Payworks-native module: *"If someone started 5 years ago, I can look in and see what the 5 reviews are, where they've progressed, what they've got." — Their HR Manager*. QuickBase does keep trending history, so this is not a gap they feel today *inside* QuickBase — it is a requirement any replacement inherits, and the reason the history must land on the Payworks employee record rather than in a second system.

**How Northwind is running reviews today.** One annual cycle, January to December, kicked off with training at the start of Q4 and targeted to complete by late January. Reviews cover salaried and hourly-admin staff only: *"We don't do it for our hourly, labor-type positions." — Their HR Manager*. The first reviewed level in the field is the route supervisor; the driver in the truck is not reviewed. Scoring is a 1–5 scale (role model at the top) applied to two rated sections — goals and performance behaviours — plus an unrated development-action-plan section. Goals carry 70% of the overall score and behaviours 30%, and that single overall number is what drives compensation. Goals cascade corporate → line-of-business/department → individual and change every year; behaviours are fixed year to year, tied to corporate values, with an extra block for people leaders. The employee self-assesses, the manager assesses, both add comments, they meet, and **the manager closes the review**. HR does not approve anything; the executive team approves the increases, and they go to the Owner. A mid-year check-in was introduced last year, manager-launched and non-mandatory.

**Opportunity Areas.** Four, in order of leverage: (1) **the reviews-to-payroll seam** — this is the account's pain and the displacement wedge; nothing about the review UI matters as much as removing the export/re-key/upload loop; (2) **the overall-score calculation** — if the system computes the weighted 70/30 score and generates the report, two of the three manual stages disappear at once; (3) **configurability of goals** — corporate goals are re-set annually and cascade three levels, so a fixed goal taxonomy fails on contact; (4) **cadence support beyond annual** — their HR Manager wants mid-year to stick and quarterly eventually, and the prototype's goal-gap detection is the affordance she reached for unprompted.

**Strategy / Hypothesis Validation.** The initiative's hypothesis is that a Payworks-native review process can displace what these accounts already run. This call supports it and sharpens it. It supports it on motivation: the account committed to the pilot before seeing anything work, and their Head of HR reordered her own departure to get the conversation started. It sharpens it on the **bar**: their Head of HR's floor is an integration, not consolidation. A Payworks review module that replicates QuickBase's review experience but keeps the payroll seam manual would fail their test; a thinner review surface that closes the seam would pass it. The competitive frame is also not what a win-loss table would suggest — the incumbent is an in-house build on a general platform, and its weakness is precisely the thing Payworks owns.

**Key Product Gaps**
- **The reviews-to-payroll seam (explicit, and the account's #1):** a completed review does not produce anything payroll can consume. They export the employee list from Payworks, re-key ratings out of QuickBase into a spreadsheet, and upload the result back into Payworks by hand.
- **No computed overall score or generated report:** the 70/30 weighted score exists only once someone assembles it. Their HR Manager's target state is that the system does it and she only inspects outliers.
- **Review history is not on the Payworks employee record:** a five-year review trail is what she expects to open from the employee file; today it lives only in a second system.
- **No 1-up visibility into a manager's ratings (implied, then stated):** a district manager cannot see how his operational managers rated their route supervisors without asking each one. This is a gap in QuickBase, not just in our prototype.
- **Off-cycle hires fall out of the goal-setting cycle (implied gap):** someone hired in April has no goals at year-end. Named by their HR Manager as *"one of the biggest misses we do have."*
- **Goal quality is a manual HR service (implied gap):** more than half of submitted goals need rewording to be measurable, and HR maintains a bank of SMART-goal phrasings to compensate. No product surface helps here today.
- **Basic authoring affordances are missing in the incumbent (implied gap, thin):** their Head of HR listed spell check and autosave as things she wants, in a five-second aside naming what QuickBase lacks. Recorded as a signal of expectation level, not as a sized request.

## Discovery Findings

The call ran half account-thread, half discovery, so the as-is process model is set out here in its own right — this is the most complete description of Northwind's review process we have from HR.

### The four-stage history

| Stage | What it was | Evidence |
|-------|-------------|----------|
| **Nothing** | No review process at all | *"before that, we didn't even have anything. They weren't doing anything" — Their HR Manager* |
| **Paper** | Paper-based reviews | *"it was just all paper-based" — Their HR Manager* |
| **Excel** | Manual re-keying into a spreadsheet; built by their HR Manager as the first structured process | *"we went from paper-based to hand-bombing everything into an Excel spreadsheet" — Their HR Manager* |
| **QuickBase (~2–3 years ago → today)** | In-house build with their IT and business-development teams; automated the review capture and keeps trending history | *"we worked together with our IT department, and we created a platform in what's called QuickBase" — Their Head of HR* |

Why QuickBase specifically: it was already in the building and their IT team could configure it. *"it's not an LMS, it's not anything like that" — Their Head of HR*. It solved the automation problem and left the integration problem untouched — *"Offered the ability to be more automated, which was great." — Their Head of HR*, but *"what it didn't offer was any, interaction with our pay system with PayWorks" — Their Head of HR*. Their standing complaints about it: *"Cubase is okay, it has limits, it loses information, it's hard to make changes, like, you have to go through several processes to make changes" — Their Head of HR*.

### Scope, cycle and cadence

- **Cycle:** *"there is a performance review cycle that's January to December." — Their Head of HR*
- **Kickoff:** training at the start of Q4, launched with company-wide webinars in November, targeted complete by late January. This year it slipped 2–3 weeks; the first online year slipped about a month and still did not reach 100%.
- **Population:** *"our performance evaluation process is just for salaried and hourly admin employees at this time. We don't do it for our hourly, labor-type positions." — Their HR Manager*
- **Mid-year:** introduced last year, in June, manager-launched, one page, and explicitly optional — *"We didn't make it mandatory, it was more, hey, we've got mid-years, have the conversations." — Their HR Manager*. Purpose: *"No one should be surprised at the end of the year when they might have fallen behind, or they didn't achieve something." — Their HR Manager*
- **Aspiration:** *"I would love to get to quarterly, but if I could get them to do it every 6 months. Bonus." — Their HR Manager*. The constraint she names is manager habit, not tooling: *"It's just getting them used to the process. Again, teaching them the value added in the process." — Their HR Manager*
- **Outside those two moments:** *"nothing that's documented to look at the performance." — Their HR Manager*. Informal check-ins happen on some teams and not others.

### The review's structure

| Section | Rated? | Weight | Notes |
|---------|--------|--------|-------|
| **Goals** | Yes, 1–5 | 70% | Set annually; cascade corporate → line-of-business/department → individual |
| **Performance behaviours** | Yes, 1–5 | 30% | Fixed year to year, *"Directly linked to our corporate values." — Their HR Manager*; an extra block for people leaders — *"if you're identified as a people leader, there's an extra part you get evaluated on." — Their HR Manager* |
| **Development action plan (DAP)** | No | — | *"It's not rated, but it is a part where you need to include what do you plan as your development for the coming year, and what did you achieve for the past year." — Their HR Manager* |

The two rated sections combine into one number: *"The goals are weighted at 70% of the overall score. And the performance behaviors are rated at 30% of the overall score, so at the end, you have an overall score based on those two ratings" — Their HR Manager* — and that number is what drives compensation.

### The goal cascade

Three levels: *"we've got our corporate goals, it cascades down to department or line of business goals… And then you could have individual goals, so it cascades down to the three levels." — Their HR Manager*. Their worked example: *"Let's use turnover, for example. Our corporate goal is, like, a 7% reduction" — Their HR Manager*, adjusted annually — *"this year it's 7%, last year it was 10%." — Their HR Manager*. One form is used across every line of business (grounds maintenance, equipment-yard operations, seasonal snow removal), which is why the corporate goal is the only constant: *"The only thing that remains the same is the corporate goal." — Their HR Manager*. Goals are agreed in conversation but the manager decides: *"They might make a suggestion, but the final agreement, of course, is the manager." — Their HR Manager*.

Goal *quality* is a standing HR workload. More than half of submitted goals are not measurable — *"how often does it happen? Probably, at least over half." — Their HR Manager* — and HR runs SMART-goal training plus *"a bank of, here's some ways you can word your SMART goals" — Their HR Manager*.

### Who reviews whom, and who approves

The chain their HR Manager described: route supervisor is the first reviewed level; a location manager reviews roughly four route supervisors plus a location admin; a fleet-maintenance manager reviews a maintenance admin; a divisional/district manager reviews roughly four operational managers. The hourly field crew is out of scope — *"the hourly person, the guy in the truck, isn't being reviewed." — Their HR Manager* — so performance signal about crews reaches the review indirectly, through the supervisor's service record, safety and property-damage record.

Approval, stated flatly and worth carrying into definition:

- The employee self-assesses and comments; the manager assesses and comments; both are meant to see each other's before the meeting — *"the theory behind it is both should be able to see what the review is" — Their HR Manager* — though in practice comments often land only at the end.
- **The manager closes the review.** *"The manager is the one who closes and completes the review." — Their HR Manager*
- **HR approves nothing.** *"HR really doesn't approve anything, we just sort of take the results of a manager who's made the call" — Their HR Manager*, and again: *"We don't approve anything." — Their HR Manager*
- **The executive team approves the increases**, and they go higher: *"On the increase, yes, that's done by the executive team." — Their HR Manager* / *"right now it goes right up to ownership." — Their HR Manager*

### Adoption reality

Completion is improving but not solved. *"the importance of it has become highlighted a lot more, so we've gotten way better this year, in getting the completion rates done" — Their HR Manager*. Manager engagement is uneven — *"it's a hit and miss. Like I said, some teams are very committed to sort of looking at them." — Their HR Manager* — and some arrive at review time with *"geez, we never set any goals." — Their HR Manager*. On the employee side: *"there's some employees that don't do the self-evaluation… that don't believe in the process, and think, we're not going to get raises anyway" — Their HR Manager*. HR's counter-move has been to push the development plan as the point of the exercise rather than the increase.

## Insights / Learnings

### The displacement bar is an integration bar, not a feature bar

What has to be true for Payworks to take this workload is narrower — and harder — than "build a better review tool".

| Insight | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Their stated minimum is a link, not consolidation** | Their Head of HR set the floor explicitly and then separately expressed consolidation as the preference. Recording both matters: an integrated-but-separate answer clears her bar, and a consolidated answer that leaves the payroll seam manual does not.<br><br>*"It doesn't need to be within our pay system, meaning not within PayWorks, but if it could at least link" — Their Head of HR* |
| **The incumbent is an in-house build, not a competitor product** | QuickBase was configured by their own IT team because it was already in the building. Nobody evaluated it against Payworks — Payworks had nothing to evaluate. That makes the switching cost political and habitual rather than contractual.<br><br>*"we worked together with our IT department, and we created a platform in what's called QuickBase" — Their Head of HR* |
| **Expectations are set by ADP and SAP, not by us** | Their Head of HR and at least one other leader came from large-suite HR systems, and that is where "why isn't this just in the pay system" comes from.<br><br>*"I have a more progressive leader that has… has come from an ADP or an SAP background, same as me, where we're like, why can't this just be in our pay system, and you just grab everything?" — Their Head of HR* |
| **Their target state is one sentence, and it is a payroll sentence** | Asked what good looks like, their Head of HR did not describe a review screen — she described the rating flowing into a report, pulling prior-year history, and attaching to a percentage increase.<br><br>*"it automatically dumps into a report, and it pulls from last year, gives you the analysis, and it'll attach to, you know, as soon as you put in the percentage increase" — Their Head of HR* |

### The manual chain between a closed review and a paycheque

This is the account's stated pain and the thread we ran out of time on.

| Insight | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Three manual stages, each with a verification step** | Export the employee list from Payworks and hand-scrub it; re-key ratings out of QuickBase into spreadsheets (or have managers do it, then verify); upload the result into Payworks to change the rating.<br><br>*"we have to look at all these spreadsheets and manually enter the data from QuickBase, or get the managers to, which, again, we have to go back and verify" — Their HR Manager* |
| **QuickBase cannot produce the artefact payroll needs** | The system holds the ratings and still cannot emit the file that changes someone's pay.<br><br>*"it still doesn't generate the report we need that we have to upload into PayWorks to change the rating of someone." — Their HR Manager* |
| **The compensation half compounds it** | Ratings are only one input; last year's increase and hire date have to be reconciled alongside them.<br><br>*"if there's any compensation bonus increases that have to go through from the ratings, and looking at what was the increase last year, when were they hired, and all this kind of stuff, it's… It's just a nightmare, as you can imagine." — Their HR Manager* |

## Slack Summary

Great first working session with Northwind Landscaping's HR leadership today on Performance Management! Full recap here: `accounts/northwind-landscaping/calls/summaries/2026-07-17.md`

**They committed to the pilot, unprompted**
Their Head of HR had a hard stop 36 minutes in and used her last minutes to say *"we want to be part of your pilot to make it right."* She named their HR Manager as the SME and builder for this, and told us to keep going without her. She also reordered her own schedule to get the conversation started before going away — *"This is how important this is to me."*

**The history of their process is the most useful thing we got**
Nothing → paper → Excel → QuickBase, over roughly three years, driven by growth. Crucially: **nobody chose QuickBase over Payworks.** Their IT team configured a general in-house platform because Payworks had nothing to choose. Their Head of HR was blunt about what it did and didn't solve — it automated the capture, and *"what it didn't offer was any, interaction with our pay system with PayWorks."*

**The displacement bar is lower and sharper than we assumed**
Their Head of HR's stated floor is not consolidation — it's integration: *"It doesn't need to be within our pay system, meaning not within PayWorks, but if it could at least link."* Consolidation is the preference, integration is the bar. A gorgeous review module that leaves the payroll seam manual fails their test. Her one-sentence target state is a payroll sentence, not a review sentence: *"it automatically dumps into a report, and it pulls from last year, gives you the analysis, and it'll attach to… as soon as you put in the percentage increase."*

**The reviews-to-payroll seam is the pain, and we ran out of time on it**
Three manual stages: export the employee list from Payworks and scrub it, re-key ratings out of QuickBase into spreadsheets, upload back into Payworks to change ratings. QuickBase *"still doesn't generate the report we need that we have to upload into PayWorks to change the rating of someone."* When Margaret named it as the pain point, the answer was *"Ugh, it's horrible."* Parked for the next call — it's now the biggest unmapped area on this account.

**Prototype: workflow screens landed, dashboards did not**
1. The goals screen was the turning point — *"these are the gold, this is neat"*, and visuals landed well
2. Goal-gap detection was the most-liked affordance; it hits a real annual failure with off-cycle hires — *"they were hired in April, and oops, we never set goals"*
3. Calendar invites from inside the tool got an unprompted *"Which is great, by the way"*
4. The HR/manager dashboard tiles were uninterpretable — goal coverage, check-in compliance and feedback activity all drew blanks, and two figures on screen didn't reconcile. *"It's hard because we're not sure what some of these metrics are."*
5. Colours clash with their stoplight convention — *"what does blue mean? Is blue good? I have no idea."*
6. Our HR approval step doesn't match their model — HR approves nothing there, the manager closes

**Two things for the record**
Their HR Manager was dismissive of mobile — *"Can't imagine doing it on a mobile"* — which is the opposite of what the field managers on this same account told us. HR is the buyer, the field is the user. And peer/360 feedback did **not** come up at all on this call, so the known HR-vs-managers contradiction on this account gets no new evidence either way.
