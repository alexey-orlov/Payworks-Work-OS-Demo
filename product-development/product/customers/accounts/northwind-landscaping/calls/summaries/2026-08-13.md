## Executive Summary

This was a ten-minute chase call, not a working session, and it should be read as one — but
it closed two open loops and opened a new workstream. Since the 2026-08-05 reverse demo, the
HR Manager has acted on her own initiative: she sent their **Controller** a note about
expense management, he is interested, and the session is already booked for end of month.
She also confirmed that the pilot group she offered is real and routine rather than
politeness on a call — *"No, that's a real thing, we do that for everything." — Their HR
Manager*. Separately, she is mid-way through Workforce Analytics 2.0 training and is
candid that she is behind on the custom-view module: *"we're doing the 2.0 modules and
there's a bit where you build your own view and I have not got that yet, but I'll get
there." — Their HR Manager*. That thread is roughly twenty-five seconds long and carries no
requirement — it is an adoption datapoint, nothing more.

This call focused on four areas: (1) the pilot team's shape and the review-cycle calendar it
has to fit into, (2) mobile access for field supervisors, (3) the Controller handoff for
expense management, and (4) — unplanned, and the most valuable part of the call — the
employee-email data gap, which she opened out into their entire onboarding process.

**Pilot Team and Cycle Timing.** Their pilot practice is genuinely mature and worth
modelling on: eight to ten people, deliberately mixed between managers and review
recipients, and — the detail that matters — *"at least one person who hates everything." —
Their HR Manager*. Asked whether that was deliberate: *"Absolutely deliberately. If you only
pilot with the enthusiastic ones you learn nothing." — Their HR Manager*. Every module of
their internal LEAD learning programme goes through this before it goes wide, and the pilot
sees it rough — *"They see it rough, before the voiceover and the animation and all that." —
Their HR Manager*. Timing is constrained: **October is out** (cycle prep); September works;
early November, just before their launch, is her preference because it would be fresh.

**The Review Cycle and Where It Stalls.** She restated the calendar precisely: launch in
November with webinars for all salaried staff, admin and leaders, and completion targeted by
end of January. It has never hit that. *"Last year we were at seventy-three percent in
February. A hundred and seventy-three completed out of — I want to say two thirty-something."
— Their HR Manager*. Critically, the failure is not the form. *"The rest are stuck at the
last step almost always. The employee's done their self-review, the manager's done theirs,
and then the manager hasn't sat down with them and closed it off. That's the bottleneck. It's
the conversation." — Their HR Manager*. Asked whether the paperwork was the problem: *"Never
the paperwork. It's booking a half hour with somebody who's on a site. And then I chase, and
then they book it, and then it gets moved." — Their HR Manager*. This is the single most
useful thing on the call for the Performance Management module: the last 27% is a scheduling
and accountability problem, not a software-completion problem.

**Escalation as the Only Nudge That Works.** Asked whether a reminder would help or just add
noise, she drew a sharp distinction: *"A nudge to the manager? Maybe. A nudge to the
manager's manager, yes. Definitely. Because the manager ignoring an email is free, and their
boss asking them about it is not free." — Their HR Manager*. And on who plays that role
today: *"It's the only thing that works. And I say that as someone who is currently the
escalation. I'm a human notification system and I'd like to retire from that role." — Their
HR Manager*. A flat reminder to the assigned manager is rated "maybe"; the escalation tier is
the definite ask.

**Mobile — Raised Again, and Now With a Worked Example.** She pre-empted the question before
Claire finished asking it: *"I know what the question is. And the answer is our route
supervisors do not have computers. I've said this to Margaret about five times and I'll keep
saying it until somebody writes it down." — Their HR Manager*. She then supplied the
strongest single piece of evidence this account has given on mobile: one of their site
supervisors ran his entire crew's reviews on paper this year, off his own initiative, for
hourly staff who are not even in scope — *"Printed them off, had the guys fill them out in
the lunchroom on a Sunday, then interviewed them an hour at a time over two days." — Their HR
Manager*. Her verdict on the cost: *"If that man had had it on his phone, he'd have done it
in half the time and I'd have the data. Instead there's a stack of paper in a yard office
somewhere and it's worth nothing to us." — Their HR Manager*. The underlying product
constraint is a role-model gap, not a screen-size gap: *"the app you've got now only knows
those guys as employees… Not as managers. So a supervisor can punch in and book a vacation
day, and that's it. He's got nine people reporting to him and the app doesn't know." — Their
HR Manager*.

**Expense / Controller Handoff.** Closed. She raised it herself: *"Oh — yes, I meant to tell
you. I sent the Controller a note after we spoke to Margaret, and he's interested. I think
there's something in the calendar for the end of the month." — Their HR Manager*. Claire
confirmed Michelle Tremblay holds it. She added a useful read on the person: *"He'll be blunt
with you, just so you know. He's not unkind but he's a numbers man, he'll tell you what he
thinks." — Their HR Manager*. **No expense requirement was stated on this call** — receipt
capture, approval thresholds and mobile expense capture were not raised, and remain
hypotheses on [expense-management-vp2](../../../../../initiatives/expense-management-vp2.md).

**The Email Gap, and What It Opened.** Their in-house QuickBase review system pulls employee
master data from Payworks and matches on the company email address on the Payworks profile —
*"That's the good bit, that's why we built it that way." — Their HR Manager*. But the field
is optional and frequently empty: *"whoever's setting them up in Payworks doesn't always put
the company email in. It's not a required field, so it just… doesn't get filled." — Their HR
Manager*. The failure surfaces months later, when a manager cannot find the employee to start
a review, and the recovery has a 24-hour tail: *"We add it, and then you have to wait
twenty-four hours for it to sync, and then it works. Every time. Same phone call, maybe
fifteen times a year." — Their HR Manager*. She is explicit that the omission is rational, not
careless — most of the two hundred–plus seasonal people hired between March and mid-May will
never use a company email. *"So the person setting them up isn't being lazy, they're being
sensible. It's the ones who get promoted later that bite us." — Their HR Manager*.

**Onboarding — Unprompted, and She Says It Is the Bigger Problem.** She asked permission to
go off-topic and then delivered the most consequential statement of the call: *"I think the
onboarding side is a bigger problem for us than the review side. I just don't bring it up
because you're here about reviews and I don't want to be that customer." — Their HR Manager*.
Claire's response — *"Please be that customer"* — plus the disclosure that she owns onboarding
as well as performance, unlocked a rapid, dense account. There is no onboarding system:
*"There's a Word document with a checklist on it, and there's a folder on the shared drive,
and there's a paper package the new person gets on day one that somebody prints." — Their HR
Manager*. Nobody owns completion: *"Nobody ticks anything. It's a Word document. It's a list
of things to remember, and if you remember them, great, and if you don't, you find out in
November when somebody can't do a review." — Their HR Manager*. The packages have silently
diverged by province — Ontario's is printed by their HR Coordinator, BC's by "whoever's
around" — and had been different for two years before she found out in June, from an employee
correcting her.

**How Northwind Runs the Employee Lifecycle Today (this call's slice).** Payworks is the
system of record for the employee, and everything downstream waits on it. A new hire fills in
the same information three times — on paper on day one, again when payroll sets them up, and
again for anything system-side, because self-service is unreachable for two weeks. That delay
is a **payroll-timing artefact**, not a policy: *"it needs their employee number, and they
don't have an employee number until they're on a pay run, and the pay run is biweekly." —
Their HR Manager*. The consequence is an inversion: *"the self-service piece, which is the
good bit, which is where they'd do their own banking and their own tax forms — they can't get
to it until after the first thing it would have helped with." — Their HR Manager*. In the
March-to-mid-May hiring window, the same failure repeats at volume and compounds with the
empty-email problem: *"in spring, when we're doing two hundred of them, it's the same problem
two hundred times. And that's the same six weeks where nobody's filling in email addresses,
so it all compounds." — Their HR Manager*.

**Strategy / Hypothesis Validation.** She stated the causal spine herself, and flagged it as
quotable: *"Everything's downstream of 'we can't do anything with this person until payroll
says they exist.' Which — I understand why. I'm not saying it's wrong. But that's the wall."
— Their HR Manager*. Claire asked to take it back to the team because *"that's very close to
something we're arguing about internally."* This is direct external evidence for the
cross-module premise of [employee-onboarding](../../../../../initiatives/employee-onboarding.md)
— that hire-to-first-pay is one flow currently split across HR, Payroll and ESS — and it is
the **second account** to speak to onboarding, closing that initiative's "one map is not a
pattern" open loop with a source that is not Maplewood.

**Opportunity Areas.** Three. (1) The review module's win condition at this account is not
the form, it is the closing conversation — an escalation-tiered nudge plus mobile capture
attacks the actual 27%. (2) Manager identity on mobile is a **shared** unlock: it is the
performance blocker, the reason a supervisor's paper reviews produced no data, and it was
raised to the expense PM by the same person for the same reason — one capability, three
module payoffs. (3) Onboarding at this account is a genuine greenfield: no incumbent to
displace, a named willing interviewee, and a customer who ranks it above the workstream we
came in on.

**UX Findings**
- **Payworks employee record — company email is optional with a silent downstream failure:**
  the field's absence is invisible at the moment of setup and only surfaces months later, in
  a different system, as "the employee doesn't come up in the list."<br><br>*"It's not a
  required field, so it just… doesn't get filled." — Their HR Manager*
- **24-hour sync tail on the fix:** once corrected, the record is not usable same-day, so the
  support call cannot be closed in one touch.<br><br>*"We add it, and then you have to wait
  twenty-four hours for it to sync, and then it works. Every time." — Their HR Manager*
- **Mobile app models a supervisor as an employee only:** a supervisor with nine direct
  reports gets a punch-in and a vacation request and nothing else.<br><br>*"He's got nine
  people reporting to him and the app doesn't know." — Their HR Manager*
- **Workforce Analytics 2.0 custom views are the drop-off point in training:** she is
  persistent and self-directed and still has not cleared the build-your-own-view
  module.<br><br>*"there's a bit where you build your own view and I have not got that yet"
  — Their HR Manager*

**Key Product Gaps**
- **No escalation tier on review reminders:** the only intervention she says works is a
  notification to the manager's manager. Today she performs that function personally, at
  scale, by hand.
- **Manager role absent from the mobile app:** the capability gap that turned a supervisor's
  self-started review round into a stack of paper in a yard office with no data behind it.
- **Company email optional on the employee record, with no downstream warning:** roughly
  fifteen support calls a year at this account alone, each with a 24-hour recovery tail.
  *Implied* gap — she described it as a nuisance and never asked for a fix, so no
  feature-request record was filed against it.
- **Self-service gated behind the first pay run:** ESS is unreachable for ~2 weeks because the
  employee number depends on a biweekly pay run, so the surface designed to collect banking
  and tax details arrives after those details have already been collected on paper. *Implied*
  gap — she accepts the constraint (*"I'm not saying it's wrong"*) rather than requesting a
  change.
- **No system of record for onboarding completion:** a Word checklist nobody ticks, a shared
  drive, and a printed package that has silently forked by province. Payworks ships
  Onboarding Checklists in Human Resources today and this account is not using them — worth
  understanding why before scoping a module.
- **No cross-province consistency check on onboarding material:** a two-year divergence
  between BC and Ontario packages surfaced only because an employee contradicted the HR
  Manager.
