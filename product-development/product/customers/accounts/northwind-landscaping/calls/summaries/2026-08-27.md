## Executive Summary

This call was a structured discovery session with Northwind's Controller — the person who owns finance, payroll reporting, and the entire expense process end to end. The HR Manager joined for the first 17 minutes and contributed two consequential points before leaving for an analytics training session. The session was the first in the design-partner corpus to go deep on expense management from the client (A/P) side, and almost every topic it covered either confirms a hypothesis, overturns one, or adds a hard constraint that wasn't visible before.

This call focused on five areas: (1) the current receipt-to-reimbursement flow and where it breaks, (2) the job-coding problem that makes receipt capture insufficient on its own, (3) the approval chain — thresholds, geography, and the gaming it currently produces, (4) payroll register integration with its two hard requirements, and (5) mobile — capture, approvals, and notifications.

**The receipt problem, quantified.** Northwind runs ~60 trucks across Ontario and BC. Fuel cards handle $28k/month cleanly (one statement). Everything else — mulch, irrigation fittings, mower belts bought at the nearest supply store — goes into a glove-box envelope. The A/P clerk (referred to throughout as "she") sorts a physical Reebok shoebox that has been the same box for four years: 650 receipts in season (May–October), 250 in February, $40k/month in season, $350k/year total (excluding fuel). She spends 2.5 days at every month end keying ~400 lines after splitting taxes and coding GL accounts and job numbers. Those 2.5 days are the worst days on the calendar because month-end close is competing for the same person. *"She's supposed to be closing the month, and she's sorting a shoebox." — Their Controller*.

**Job coding is the real problem, and it is a revenue problem.** The Controller's sharpest insight: photo capture solves the paper but not the coding. The crew member at the counter knows the site ("north arena") but not the contract number ("2019-14"). The clerk has a sheet and does the translation three weeks later, off a receipt that may be unreadable from sitting on a July dashboard. If she gets it wrong — *"there's no error, there's just a slightly smaller invoice to the municipality and everyone goes home happy."* Northwind has 11 municipal contracts where materials are billable to the client. A miscoded receipt is silent revenue leakage. The Controller's requirement for mobile capture is not the photo — the photo is table stakes. The requirement is site attribution at the moment of capture.

**The approval chain has BC as a structural exception.** Ontario has regional managers; BC does not — BC yards report straight to the Ops Director. The Ontario chain applied to BC routes approvals through a person who doesn't exist. The current four-tier chain ($500 / $2,500 / $5,000, with Owner above $5,000) was designed around round numbers ("people actually follow rules they can remember") rather than policy science, and the Controller knows this. He also knows the per-report threshold creates gaming: supervisors split submissions across two months to stay under the tier. He doesn't call it fraud. He calls it practical, and he wants the product to handle it differently.

**The payroll register requirement is a hard compliance constraint.** The Controller would take expense reimbursement on the payroll run — but only under two conditions he described as "hard." First, reimbursements must be non-taxable: off the T4, off CPP pensionable earnings, off EI insurable earnings. *"If it does that once we'd have a very bad year."* Second, he approves the payroll register before transmitting. If expenses can enter a run after he signs, he hasn't actually approved it. He needs expenses to appear on the register before lock — total per employee, clickable to the underlying receipts — and to lock with the rest of the register. Expenses that miss the cutoff roll to the next run automatically; the submitter sees the new target date.

**Status visibility is the single highest-volume pain.** The Controller gets more phone calls about expenses than about payroll. The question is never "can you approve this" — it is "did my envelope even arrive." His formulation: *"He doesn't need it faster, necessarily. He needs to know."* The HR Manager generalised this across every HR process: reviews, time off, expenses — same question every time. Status visibility ("approved, paying on the eleventh") is described as eliminating 90% of the phone calls.

**They evaluated a standalone expense tool last year and rejected it.** The reason: $9/user/month × 200-seat minimum = $21–22k/year, which he could defend against 2.5 days of clerk time. What he couldn't defend: it didn't talk to payroll, and it didn't know job numbers — he'd still be exporting CSVs and re-keying into job costing. *"So I'd be paying twenty-two grand to move the problem."* This is direct evidence for why payroll-native expense management is the value proposition: not feature parity with standalone tools, but integration with the data the client already has.

**Commercial signal.** The Controller disclosed that Northwind's renewal is "on the horizon" and he would rather be adding a module than renegotiating the same price for the same scope. He named Amanda as the account owner and deferred the renewal date to her. Michelle will flag this conversation to Amanda.

**Opportunity Areas.** Four. (1) Receipt capture + site attribution at the moment of purchase closes the job-coding gap that standalone tools cannot solve — this is the technical moat. (2) Position-based approval routing removes the maintenance burden that makes the current Word document a liability; BC vs. Ontario geography is the first differentiator an enterprise would price separately. (3) Payroll register integration with a non-taxable EFT line creates a direct-debit path the hourly workforce would feel immediately. (4) Status visibility is the cheapest win: a four-state status display and a pay-date label may eliminate more friction than any other single feature on this call.

**UX Findings**
- **Mobile capture — offline is the blocker, not the screen size:** dead zones exist past the escarpment in Ontario and in BC. If the app returns "no connection, try again," the user closes it and the receipt is gone. The offline queue with a visible count ("3 waiting to send") is the trust mechanism.<br><br>*"It cannot say 'no connection, try again.' Because he's going to close it and get in the truck and the receipt is now gone." — Their Controller*
- **Mobile approvals — attention is the real constraint, not security:** the Controller will "tap yes in a parking lot" for a $2,500 claim if the app lets him. He wants the system to prevent this, not just warn him.<br><br>*"I'd tap yes. In a parking lot. I know myself. And then it's not really an approval, it's a formality, and I've built a control that doesn't control anything, which is worse than not having it." — Their Controller*
- **Notification channel — push badge, not email, for field workers:** half of the field workers have no company email in Payworks. An email notification strategy misses the exact population that most needs to know when their expense is approved.<br><br>*"If your notification story is email, it doesn't reach the people who most need it." — Their Controller*
- **Approval chain — position-binding is the ergonomics ask:** configuring the chain today requires editing a Word document and remembering to update it when people change roles. The system should derive approvers from the HR org chart automatically.<br><br>*"Nobody should have to remember." — Their Controller*

**Key Product Gaps**
- **No site/job attribution at point of capture:** the crew member knows the site name; the contract number lives in a clerk's head and a sheet. Digital capture without site selection at the counter produces a digital shoebox.
- **Approval chain is person-bound, not position-bound:** the Word document that defines routing is updated manually, named by individual, and breaks silently when someone changes roles.
- **No BC-specific routing:** BC yards cannot use the Ontario approval chain (no regional manager in BC); today this is a known workaround, not a solved problem.
- **Per-report threshold invites gaming:** submitting twice to stay under a tier is known, tolerated, and evidence that the threshold scope (per-report vs. per-receipt) is not working as intended.
- **Expense not on payroll register:** the Controller cannot see what he is approving on the payroll run until he goes to a different system; he cannot lock the register knowing it is complete.
- **No status visibility for submitters:** the single highest-volume pain point, named by both the Controller and the HR Manager independently.
- **~200 crew members cannot submit:** purchases are made by crew members but submitted under the supervisor's name. The person who spent the money cannot be reimbursed directly.
- **Push notifications absent for workers without company email:** a notification gap that compounds the status-visibility gap.

### Job Coding — the Revenue Problem

This insight reframes receipt capture as a revenue-protection feature, not an accounting convenience.

| Insight | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Eleven municipal contracts where materials are billable** | If a receipt is coded to the wrong job number, the client is not invoiced. There is no downstream check.<br><br>*"It's not a cost thing, it's a revenue thing. If that bag of grass seed doesn't get coded to the right job number, we don't bill for it. | **The crew member knows the site name, not the contract number** | Site-name-to-contract-number translation is the clerk's job, done three weeks after the purchase from a faded slip.<br><br>*"He knows the site. ### Payroll Register Integration

Two hard constraints, clearly stated, with regulatory implications.

| Insight | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Non-taxable treatment is a go/no-go condition** | The Controller named T4, CPP, and EI explicitly. Getting this wrong once has payroll compliance implications for every employee on the run.<br><br>*"If it does that once we'd have a very bad year." — Their Controller* |
| **Register lock is the harder ask** | He approves and transmits the register. Post-lock expense additions mean his approval is meaningless. The feature has to be locked AND visible before lock.<br><br>*"This is the one I actually lose sleep over." — Their Controller* |
| **Cutoff rollover must be automatic and visible to the submitter** | Payroll cutoff: Monday noon, pay Thursday. If an expense misses cutoff, it rolls silently; the submitter sees the new pay date in the app.<br><br>*"It should roll to the next one. Quietly, automatically, and the guy should be able to see, in whatever app he's got, 'approved, paying on the eleventh.'" — Their Controller* |

### Mobile — Capture vs. Approvals vs. Notifications

Three separate mobile problems with different answers.

| Insight | Details |
|---------|---------|

A desktop capture flow will produce zero captures from field workers.<br><br>*"The picture has to happen on a phone at the counter, there is no other place it can happen, if you make that a web form on a desktop nothing will ever be captured." — Their Controller* |

| **Push badge, not email, for field approvers** | Many field workers have no company email in Payworks. This is the same data gap raised in the performance-management context — one platform problem, two module impacts. |
