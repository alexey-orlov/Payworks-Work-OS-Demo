## Executive Summary

Their HR Manager opened by confirming she had nothing further from the previous prototype walkthrough — *"I don't think there was anything that sort of really stood out. I mean, it was pretty quick that we kind of went through it" — Their HR Manager* — and then took the call in the direction she had proposed in advance: *"I wanted to give you a demo a little bit of… this is what it looks like." — Their HR Manager*. She shared her screen for roughly the next fifty minutes and walked the entire annual performance and merit cycle, from the launch email through to the payroll upload. This is the single most complete current-state record we hold for any design partner in the Performance Management discovery, and it is the record that shows where the pain actually sits.

This call focused on four areas: (1) the review cycle as it runs in QuickBase today, (2) the merit workflow that follows it — the spreadsheet chain that is the account's defining pain, (3) the pilot-group commitment for prototype testing, and (4) a short expense-management thread at the very end that produced the only first-hand expense evidence in this corpus.

**The review itself is not the problem — and that matters.** QuickBase is a platform they build on themselves, in its third year for performance reviews and iterated every cycle off manager feedback. The form has five behaviour ratings (six for people leaders, the sixth on leadership skills) on a 1–5 scale with mandatory tangible examples at 1 and 5; a goal-review tab that auto-carries last year's objectives forward; a development-opportunities section wired to their internal LEAD leadership programme; and a next-year goal-setting tab. A summary tab auto-computes the overall score — behaviours weighted 30%, goals 70%, unchanged for as long as she has been there, inherited from the Excel workbook that preceded it. Manager and employee complete the *identical* form; the manager's copy is the one that becomes final. Reactions are genuinely positive: *"Some really like it compared to what we were doing before. Like I said with the Excel spreadsheet and everything else, it's a lot more defined." — Their HR Manager*. We should not plan to win on the form.

**The merit workflow is where the account bleeds, and she said so before we asked.** Her own framing when Margaret asked what was not working: *"Aha! So here's the biggest challenge. I mean, this is… this is a bit barbaric to what I know you can create for us." — Their HR Manager*. The full sequence is in the Insights section below and should be read in order — the short version is that QuickBase cannot export and cannot produce a report, so every score in the company is re-keyed by hand into a spreadsheet, reconciled by eye, reviewed by the executive team, signed by the Owner, and then re-uploaded into Payworks by the Payroll Administrator against a hard cutoff. Her verdict on the artefact at the centre of it: *"It's gotta be the worst spreadsheet you've ever experienced." — Their HR Manager*, and later, *"it's that whole other nightmare of trying to get these scores, and then next year we're gonna do the exact same thing." — Their HR Manager*.

**The headline ask is consolidation, not a new review tool.** She stated it plainly: *"for 5 years, I should be able to go right into PayWorks. Not only can I see your whole file, now I can see your performance file." — Their HR Manager*. Today there is a four-year hole: when reviews were on paper and Excel they were printed, signed, scanned and filed into the employee's Payworks record — *"But for the last 4 years since we don't do paper, yeah, you got nothing." — Their HR Manager*. Margaret's read back to her: *"So now you have two records to…"*. And on why an in-Payworks solution is the answer — *"There's no way to have it captured in PayWorks, where it's assigned to an employee." — Their HR Manager*, answered with *"Absolutely."* when Margaret named the benefit of solving it inside Payworks.

**The pilot is committed.** She adopted our pilot-group suggestion immediately and shaped it herself: six people, deliberately split east and west, mixing managers and employees — *"A manager from the east, a manager from the West, an employee from the East, an employee from the West." — Their HR Manager*. This is a pattern she already runs — *"I've got a number of pilot teams out there that help us create stuff and everything we look at" — Their HR Manager* — and the same practice governs every LEAD training module before release. She proposed the structure: one kickoff session with all six, then split 3/3 across the two prototypes with separate sessions each, and she volunteered to bring the manager subgroup back specifically for the merit step — *"we'll still use those same managers to walk us through How they find that horrible process." — Their HR Manager*.

**Expense management — a short, narrow, and genuinely useful thread.** With under three minutes of the call left, Margaret asked whether they were satisfied with expenses today and whether expenses go through payroll. The exchange that followed runs roughly ninety seconds of substance (transcript 01:05:38 → 01:07:33) and is recorded in full in its own Insights section below, because it is the only first-hand expense evidence in the entire design-partner corpus. It establishes three things, and precision about them matters: claims route claimant → Head of HR approval → **accounts payable**, explicitly *not* payroll; the claimant herself is satisfied; and the **Controller** — who payroll reports to, and who she named as *"the main contact here at Northwind Landscaping with PayWorks"* — was offered as the next conversation. It establishes nothing about receipts, mobile capture, corporate cards, thresholds, volumes, or cycle times. See the caution in that section.

**How Northwind is using Payworks around this workflow.** Payworks is the system of record for the employee master data the review process depends on: QuickBase's drop-downs pull employee records from Payworks over an API their own IT development team built, and payroll produces the salaried extract that seeds the merit spreadsheet and the increases that land back in it. That dependency is also a failure mode — if a new employee's company email address is missing from the Payworks profile the pull silently returns nothing, HR's first diagnostic step is to open the Payworks file and check, and after adding it there is a 24-hour wait. She closed the call describing how much else is in flight on their side: *"we've got calls going also with the analytics… the 2.0, so we're doing training on that, and we want to look at the document management part of things, and I'm using the file and fill form, and yeah, I'm pretty well So involved in PayWorks right now" — Their HR Manager*.

**Opportunity Areas.**
1. **Own the handoff, not the form.** The wedge at this account is the step QuickBase cannot do at all — carrying a completed score into the merit decision and then into payroll. Everything upstream of that they already like.
2. **Put the performance record on the employee file.** This is the stated ask, it is cheap to describe, and it closes a four-year visibility hole they can name precisely.
3. **Cycle-level orchestration.** Launch, chase, and close-off are manual today (email, webinars, phone calls); 27% of the 2025 cycle was still open months after target. A system that launches the cycle for everyone and chases the stragglers addresses their loudest operational complaint after the spreadsheet.
4. **Take the Controller introduction seriously and quickly.** It is the only route we have to A/P-side expense evidence, and it was offered unprompted.

**Strategy / Hypothesis Validation.** The Performance Management discovery is testing whether a Payworks-native review process can displace what design partners already run. This call sharpens that hypothesis rather than confirming it wholesale. The incumbent here is a *good enough* self-built form with three years of iteration behind it and real user goodwill — so displacement will not be won on review-form quality. It will be won on the two things the incumbent structurally cannot do: move a score into compensation and payroll without a human retyping it, and keep the review on the employee's record. That is a narrower and more defensible claim than "build performance management," and this record is the strongest evidence for it we have.

It also adds a **fourth distinct compensation model** to the set the initiative page already tracks: a company-wide merit pool (*"2.5% or 3%"*), a nominally straight rating-to-percentage guideline, a per-manager total percentage that cannot be exceeded, and explicit manager discretion to depart from the recommendation. Per the initiative's instructions this divergence is reported, not smoothed — there is still no single compensation model to build against.

**UX Findings**
- **Cycle launch is per-manager and manual:** no manager triggers at the same time, and an employee cannot start their own review. Reviews sit unstarted until HR notices.<br><br>*"it'll be halfway through that review period we have set up. And we'll go in and we'll say, oh my gosh, Johnny Manager hasn't even started his reviews. He hasn't even launched them for the self-reviews to start." — Their HR Manager*
- **Manager copy vs. self-review copy was indistinguishable:** a real early failure they patched with in-form labels and reminder text.<br><br>*"you didn't know if you were working in the manager's copy or the self-review copy, which made it really challenging. So, we added some stuff here, again, all these reminders about what QuickBase doesn't do for you." — Their HR Manager*
- **No autosave, no spell check:** the first year cost people their work.<br><br>*"Some of the challenges with QuickBase is it does not have a spell check, and it does not autosave. So, first year, huge challenge. People weren't saving their work, and they lost it, which… Literally pissed a lot of people off." — Their HR Manager*
- **No override on the computed score:** the summary tab is pure arithmetic, so a manager who dislikes the final number has to go back and change the underlying ratings rather than adjust the result. Margaret named it and she agreed — *"Go back and play." — Their HR Manager*
- **Development plan is employee-only, but the manager's copy is the final record** — so the section cannot land on the record that counts. She has already flagged it internally.<br><br>*"I want both manager and employee to have that opportunity, because some employees are doing it, and they are a member of the LEAD program… Because the manager's is the final, I want the manager to have that opportunity also." — Their HR Manager*
- **Employee master data fails silently:** a missing company email in the Payworks profile means the employee simply will not pull, surfacing as a manager complaint rather than an error.<br><br>*"So, someone will say, hey, that guy's… I can't get my employee to pull up." — Their HR Manager*
- **History is per-person and trapped:** past reviews are visible in QuickBase one employee at a time, so every manager re-opens QuickBase per report every cycle.<br><br>*"every manager's got to kind of go back in and look into QuickBase to see what those scores are." — Their HR Manager*
- **Mobile is available but cramped** — asked, and answered as a current-state fact, not raised as a complaint or a request: *"No, you can access it on your mobile device. You can get into QuickBase. It's just a lot smaller." — Their HR Manager*

**Key Product Gaps**
- **No score handoff from review to compensation:** the entire spreadsheet chain exists only because a completed score cannot move. QuickBase has no export — *"it's not… is it not… it's not exportable today from QuickBase?" — Margaret Foster* / *"No, it's not." — Their HR Manager* — so every rating in the company is retyped by a manager into a spreadsheet tab.
- **No review history on the employee record:** four years of completed reviews are unreachable from the Payworks file; the scanned paper era is still there, everything since is not. The explicit ask.
- **No cycle-level launch:** reviews are created one at a time by individual managers. She named the fix herself as desirable — *"if it was an automatic trigger for all managers to say the review period has started, and poof, the system could generate it itself, that would be a great thing also."*
- **No completion chase:** HR runs a "who is missing a review" report and then follows up by email, phone and webinars. 173 reviews completed = 73%; the balance is stuck at the manager's final close-off step, months past a target of end-January / early-February.
- **No reconciliation between the review system and the merit sheet:** the check is a human reading two columns. *"By just looking at the spreadsheet." … "For all employees, one by one." … "Just going through… it's just very manual."* And whether the sheet is ever verified against QuickBase is unknown even to her — *"I don't know if she actually does that, or if she actually just relies on what the manager puts in as the scoring."*
- **No merit-budget tooling:** a manager's allocation against their fixed total percentage lives in spreadsheet cells, with the constraint enforced by arithmetic and conversation.
- **No approval trail:** the executive review is an emailed file and the Owner's sign-off is a signature on a spreadsheet.
- **Manual payroll-side handoff:** the approved sheet goes back to payroll, and the Payroll Administrator keys the increases and retros into Payworks against a hard cutoff. Implied gap — an approved increase should not need re-entry into the system that produced the extract in the first place.
- **Goal cascade lives outside the review:** corporate goals are set by the ownership/executive team, sent to HR, and hand-configured into QuickBase by their IT developer; department goals are typed into open fields; a separate three-tier cascade tool is used by some departments. Nothing links a corporate goal to the individual goal that inherits it.
- **Eligibility rules are enforced by eye:** the June 1 employment cutoff is applied by the Head of HR reading hire dates and rate-change dates off the payroll extract, row by row.

### The Merit Workflow, End to End

This is the account's defining pain and the reason the call happened. The sequence below is the order she walked it, with her timings.

| Insight | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Step 0 — the cycle starts outside any system, with communication** | An email under the Head of HR's signature announces the review period, followed by three or four training webinars over Teams. Attendance is *"highly encouraged"* but not mandatory; it is tracked, and the tracking is never used — *"It's information only. Yeah, we don't do anything with it."* Corporate goals are communicated in that webinar and, officially, nowhere else.<br><br>*"when we launch the performance review process, we do webinars… a series of 3 or 4 webinars, attend one, and that kind of launches it with… then we say, this is the target date to have all your reviews done." — Their HR Manager* |
| **Step 1 — reviews are completed and closed in QuickBase, in January** | Manager launches, employee self-reviews, manager completes the identical form and closes it off. 6. Tabs come back one email at a time and get reassembled by hand
7. Eligibility (employed before June 1) is applied by reading hire dates row by row
8. The Head of HR reconciles rating against increase **by eye, one employee at a time** — *"it's just very manual"* — and nobody knows whether the sheet is ever verified against QuickBase, because no report exists
9. Executive team reviews, **the Owner signs personally**
10. The signed sheet goes back to payroll and the Payroll Administrator re-uploads it into Payworks
