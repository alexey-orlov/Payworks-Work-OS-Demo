# Maplewood Recreation (maplewood-recreation) — Account Context

> Design-partner account. Every claim below traces to the research record listed under
> History. That record was anonymized on import — company, site and person names are
> fictionalized labels, and customer-side people appear as role titles only. The record
> is thin on firmographics: it never states headcount, sites, province or union status,
> and those gaps are marked as such below rather than filled in.

## Who they are

- **Two workforces under one payroll: permanent and seasonal.** The split drives everything — *"We have permanent employees, where our annual performance review is mandatory, and then we have seasonal employees, where it's… strongly recommended, but it's not mandatory."*
- **Board-and-executive governance.** The **board of directors approves the annual budget** (*"once the budget's approved by, our board of directors and our executive team"*); everything downstream — the per-employee increase list, bonus amounts, approvals — is decided by the **executive team**, not the board.
- **A deliberately disconnected HR operating model:** *"our structure, our HR model, is we're actually quite disconnected. Our role isn't to keep track of these types of initiatives or processes. We create them…"* HR designs and trains, people leaders execute, and nobody tracks completion.
- **Microsoft-centric**: Outlook, Office 365, Teams, plus a company intranet used for form submission and document storage. Reviews run on the **fiscal** year with a **May** execution window, not the calendar year.
- **High leader turnover and a wide technology-comfort spread** — *"we have leaders that won't touch computers with a 10 feet foot pole, but we expect them to follow processes."* Their design method follows from it: *"I always think of my one or two leaders that we struggle with the most, and build the process based on their thought process."*
- Headcount, site count, province and union status are **not stated on the record**. The size band and ARR in [portfolio.yaml](../portfolio.yaml) are illustrative — see the conventions block at the top of that file.

## Current truth

- **Payworks carries payroll and employee self-service.** Completed reviews are uploaded into the ESS document library, where retrieval is a known irritation — *"we have to kind of click and click and click and click until we actually find it."* The Payroll Manager processes increases, bonuses and retro pay, and describes the team as *"very checklist… checklist-driven."*
- **They tried the existing Payworks review capability and abandoned it** — *"we actually tested out how the module's set up right now in PayWorks with our annual reviews a few years ago, but once it's submitted, it just says, yes, they get their raise."* An automatic raise-on-submit is incompatible with a board-set, executive-decided increase, and that incompatibility is the reason performance work left the product.
- **Compensation is fully decoupled from the review.** The annual increase is a list: *"I get the list of every single permanent employee, and that's what they get"* — *"So the leader doesn't get to choose or recommend or say yes or no to an increase."* Confirmed directly: the performance review *"doesn't come into play at all with the merit increase."*
- **The pay-for-performance bonus is where the review does bite.** Leaders nominate on a form uploaded to the intranet; executive approves or denies; and *"they don't look at any bonuses until the review is done"* — *"there's a dependency, for sure."* Below director level the bonus is a flat amount set by executive; directors get a percentage of salary against goal achievement, calculated at executive level.
- **They went backwards to PDF two weeks before the interview**, for two reasons both worth remembering: leaders were submitting blank digital forms, and *"personal and confidential forms were living in the back end, where system administrators that aren't privy to that confidential information could actually access"* them. Confidentiality is now the gate on any replacement — and the reason Payworks is attractive at all: *"we know that it is, built for… with the confidentiality piece around it."*
- **Onboarding and offboarding are a live, separate thread.** The Payroll Manager sent Payworks *"a massive Excel spreadsheet"* mapping onboarding; the offboarding equivalent is promised and unfinished, held up by seasonal volume — *"We just did 300 plus on the SPR side, so we're just trying to do ROEs and get everything all dialed up for that."* This exchange is the origin of [employee-onboarding](../../../initiatives/employee-onboarding.md).
- **Asks:** word-based customizable rating scales (never numeric — *"Numbers we find a bit inconsistent"*), no overall rating, per-competency comment boxes, per-field mandatory control, two form types (permanent with goals, seasonal without), goal-achievement tracking to close the loop, Outlook calendar integration for recurring 1:1s, executive dashboard access rolled up by hierarchy branch, and process-status analytics with CSV export — *not* people analytics.
- **Risks:** adoption is the headline — *"I'm just a smidgen overwhelmed… it's busy… I don't really know where to start."* Managers cannot always tell which of their own people are seasonal. Any rollout has to be staged: *"we would need to, slowly roll it out, and not go, like, super big with it."*
- **Expansion signal:** they want to consolidate into Payworks (*"I like the idea of keeping it within, our pay work system"*), and offered to bring their Employee Experience Director — *"she has a really keen eye for technology and adopting technology"* — into the next session.
