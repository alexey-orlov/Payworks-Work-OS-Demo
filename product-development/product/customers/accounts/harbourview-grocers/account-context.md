# Harbourview Grocers (harbourview-grocers) — Account Context

> Design-partner account. Every claim below traces to one of the research records listed
> under History. Those records were anonymized on import — company, site and person names
> are fictionalized labels, and customer-side people appear as role titles only.

## Who they are

- **Family-owned grocery retailer in British Columbia** — *"our company is kind of a different company because it's a family-owned business."* Eight stores plus a warehouse and a head office, with **two more stores opening next year**: *"we've got 8 stores in a warehouse. We're… next year, we're adding 2 more stores."* Store departments: meats, deli, grocery, produce, bakery, cash.
- **Just over 500 employees**, and growing — *"I have over 500 employees."* (Payworks' own records put them at *"over 587"*; the customer states the rounder figure.)
- **No HR department.** The HR function is carried three ways: the **Office Manager** is the administrator — *"I'm the office manager, as well as the HR and payroll administrator"* — the **CFO owns HR decisions** (*"we don't have an HR… specific person… our CFO does that"*), and outside counsel plus an on-call HR specialist backstop the hard cases.
- Deep hierarchy for the size: owners → COO/CFO → two district managers → store managers → department managers → assistant department managers → shift managers → CSL2 → CSL1 → ACE (Ambassador of Customer Service). Produce and meat **specialists** sit in on department-manager reviews.
- Self-image lags the size, and it shapes every requirement: *"we still run as if we're a really small company."*

## Current truth

- **Payworks carries payroll, employee documents and timesheets**; analytics adoption has just begun (*"we've just started using the analytics… piece"*); reviews are scheduled and filed in Payworks (a six-month review is set per employee, and a due list is pulled monthly). They also still depend on a **legacy, unsupported Awards module** to pay a $50 mystery-shopper award — *"Use one that's not supported anymore… but we still use it."*
- **The wage ladder is the pay system.** A "Growth and Opportunities" document sets a minimum and maximum per position, and every review triggers the step for that position unless the employee has maxed out: CSL1 **20¢** (band $18.00–$18.40), CSL2 **20¢** (band $18.50–$20.00), shift manager **25¢**, assistant department manager **40¢**, department manager **50¢**; ACE gets nothing beyond the BC minimum-wage lift each June.
- **The increase is not tied to the rating** — the sharpest finding in the account. Asked what earns the 20¢: *"No, they automatically — it's terrible. They automatically get an increase."* And on what the rating is for: *"I agree. Like it's not based on monetary at all."* The rating exists to correct promotion decisions, not pay.
- **Ratings are words, not numbers** — the BOS scale, *"beginning, owning, and sharing"*, replacing an earlier 1–5 because *"We didn't feel like we were getting… the true picture."*
- **Reviews run on hire dates, not a cycle** — 3-month probation review (no increase), first official review at month 9, then every six months, with promotions resetting the clock. *"we don't have fixed dates, and we don't have anniversary dates."* Roughly **1,000 review setups a year**, each costing her ~10 minutes of admin on top of the manager's 20–30-minute session.
- **DocuSign is the review tool, and it is a metered cost.** *"every envelope we use with DocuSign, we pay for"* — roughly *"$3,000 for 500 envelopes"* on her own hedged estimate, and voided envelopes are not refunded (a contract change last week cost two). Consolidating reviews into Payworks reduces envelope volume but does not eliminate it: employment agreements stay on DocuSign.
- **Asks, in her order:** the ability to build their own position-specific templates (*"just the ability to be able to set it up"* — the stated adoption condition), employees completing reviews in the mobile app, review-due notifications to anyone with employee access, attribution on check-ins, and a guard-rail alert on the "send to payroll" checkbox — an unticked box silently withholds an increase.
- **Risks:** cost decides, and the CFO owns it — *"It'll always come down to cost."* She is not the decision-maker, senior leadership wants more depth than the prototype showed, and store adoption is already uneven (some stores still hand-write reviews). Key-person risk is real and self-reported: *"it's getting to that max-out point."* Support responsiveness is an open irritation — *"I don't like the support system anymore."*
