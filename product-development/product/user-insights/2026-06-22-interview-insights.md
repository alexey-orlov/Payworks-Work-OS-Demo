## Executive Summary

- **The 1–5 scale is the single thing all three sessions broke on.** Employees rate
  themselves 4s and 5s, managers rate the same people 2s and 3s, and nobody has a shared
  definition of what a number means. One manager spends the review cycle writing the
  justification the employee never wrote; another says the argument is *always* about a 5.
- **The evidence a review is built from lives nowhere the review can reach it** — a paper
  journal colour-coded by department, a misplaced notepad, emails sent to oneself, monthly
  folders on a laptop, a goals spreadsheet with no link to the review document. Three of
  three. This, not the form, is where the manager's time goes.
- **Nothing chases anyone.** Reminders come from the HR Manager's inbox and travel
  manager → supervisor by nagging. Two of three asked for automated reminders unprompted;
  the third keeps a calendar entry with a daily repeat.
- **HR and the managers want opposite things from peer feedback, and the buyer is HR.**
  Two managers want co-worker and cross-department input; the HR Manager explicitly
  declines it and wants one-up-manager collaboration instead — *"we don't plan on going
  down that route."* Do not resolve this by averaging.
- **The prototype landed well on substance.** *"You sold me. I'm good to go."* — Their
  District Manager. *"I see it better than what we're using already."* — Their Regional
  Operations Manager. Every one of them is displacing QuickBase, and none of them likes it.

## Top Pain Points (ranked)

1. **The 1–5 scale means different things to the employee and the manager** — 3 of 3
   interviews — *"most employees are a 2 or 3 in reality, based on how we've… how we've been
   instructed how to do the reviews. Most employees will give themselves 4s… 4 or 5s."* —
   Their Regional Operations Manager — impact: the review conversation becomes
   an argument about the number instead of the work; *"it's usually the fives that cause the
   problem"* — workaround: managers absorb it by talking the employee down in the meeting,
   and one writes a written rebuttal for every section.
2. **The evidence a review is built from lives outside every system** — 3 of 3 interviews —
   *"I have a notebook of… Every… almost like a journal."* — Their Regional Operations
   Manager, who confirmed it is all on paper — impact: the annual review is reconstructed from memory and
   scattered paper, six to twelve months after the fact — workaround: personal monthly
   folders (interview 1), a paper notepad plus emails sent to oneself (interview 2), a
   colour-coded paper journal with weekly summaries (interview 3). Only *disciplinary*
   documents reliably reach Payworks, as uploaded PDFs.
3. **Nothing reminds a manager or an employee that a review is due** — 2 of 3 interviews —
   *"we don't get, like, automated replies saying, hey, you have this to do, make sure it's
   done by this date, kind of thing. So it'd be nice to have some automated reminders."* —
   Their District Manager — impact: cycles slip for months and the deadline is
   theatre — *"On the surface, yes. In reality, no… it's always extended every year"* (Their
   Regional Operations Manager) — workaround: the HR Manager emails, managers chase their
   supervisors, one manager sets a calendar entry with a daily repeat.
4. **Cross-department contributions are invisible to the person writing the review** —
   2 of 3 interviews — *"administrative staff reports to the one manager, but I have feedback
   on her as well, that I can't… that's not cur… that's not currently… acknowledged anywhere
   realistically, unless I tell it to him directly, or I make it a point of bringing it up."* — Their Regional Operations Manager — impact: praise
   evaporates and only problems get escalated (*"Generally, it's gonna be… it's gonna be
   negative"*) — workaround: a phone call at review time, or nothing.
5. **Goals are retyped per person instead of set once for the site** — 1 of 3 interviews
   (the district manager, the only participant carrying a full team of ~12) — *"I may have
   four at one site. They all got the same goals, but then when I'm going in, I'm typing
   that goal for every single person. So it takes a long time."* — Their District Manager —
   impact: the largest single block of review-cycle time for a multi-site manager —
   workaround: type it out again for each person, flipping between screens to keep the
   wording identical. Corroborated in shape by interview 3, where goals are maintained in
   *"an Excel sheet that I… I maintain and see that is nowhere related to it whatsoever, to
   the actual performance review document."*
6. **A rating with no explanation forces the manager to write both sides** — 1 of 3
   interviews, but the most concrete time cost anyone described — *"It ends up being more
   work on me in the end, in the end for me specifically because I will go through every
   section and I'll put, this is why you get a three instead of a five"* — Their Regional
   Operations Manager — impact: a one-line rating turns into paragraphs — workaround: he
   writes the justification himself, or the exercise is *"a redundant exercise in the end."*
7. **The score's path to a pay increase is a spreadsheet the manager never sees** — 1 of 3
   interviews (plus a corroborating "not me" from the district manager) — *"And then all
   salary increases aren't done at any of the site levels or even myself. That's done by pay
   works or by payroll."* — Their HR Manager, who added a beat later *"They then upload it
   all in."* — impact: the
   review's business outcome is opaque to the person who wrote it; the regional operations
   manager could not say what happens after submission at all: *"I assume if it's either
   excessively good or bad, then HR does something with it. I haven't experienced either of
   those."* — workaround: HR builds the spreadsheet, the executive level sets the
   percentage, payroll uploads.
8. **Managers cannot see whether an employee has actually started their self-review** —
   1 of 3 interviews — *"we don't know when they've opened it or if they've started it…
   it'll say the employee started, but starting might mean that they actually opened it, but
   they haven't done anything yet either"* — Their District Manager — impact: chasing is
   blind — workaround: open QuickBase and guess.
9. **Preparing a review means hopping between Payworks sections** — 1 of 3 interviews —
   *"So I have to go to attendance management section, then I have to go to the document
   section to pull up the folders. So if we can get a summary, that might be easier."* —
   Their Operations Manager — impact: friction rather than blockage; he was explicit that
   keeping notes outside the system is *"Not really"* a problem for him today — workaround:
   open both sections plus his own notes.

## Interviews

### Interview — HR Manager & Operations Manager (site-level operations, one region), 2026-06-22

**Research goal:** How does a site-level manager actually run the review, and what happens
to the number afterwards?
**Hypotheses tested:** that the self-review is meaningfully used by the manager; that the
manager owns the pay outcome; that notes kept during the year feed the review.

**Jobs-to-be-Done:**
When I sit down to review a supervisor at the end of the year, I want every fact about them
— discipline, attendance, certifications, the goals I set — in one place inside Payworks, so
I can rate them defensibly without opening three systems and my own folders.

**Pain points:**

- **Preparing a review means hopping between Payworks sections** — severity: medium —
  workaround: open Attendance Management, then Employee Documents, then his own notes.<br>
  *"So I have to go to attendance management section, then I have to go to the document
  section to pull up the folders. So if we can get a summary, that might be easier."* — Their
  Operations Manager
- **Year-round performance notes live in personal folders** — severity: medium — workaround:
  monthly notes kept on his own machine; he was explicit this is not currently a blocker.<br>
  *"So, we keep it on monthly basis, like, in our folders, basically. We don't put it in
  Payworks or QuickBase."* — Their Operations Manager
- **Self-ratings run high across the board** — severity: high — workaround: the manager
  re-rates from his own judgement and the pair meet to settle it.<br>
  *"So basically what I have seen is everybody would rate themselves higher for everything,
  right? We get that."* — Their Operations Manager
- **The overall number is computed off-screen** — severity: high — workaround: the manager
  submits component ratings and never sees the weighted result; HR assembles it.<br>
  *"The final number is weighted based on the first section we do, which is the performance
  behaviors is weighted at 30%, and the achievement of the goals is weighted at 70% to give
  us our overall total at the end."* — Their HR Manager
- **The merit spreadsheet is HR-and-executive-only, and payroll is the last hop** —
  severity: high — workaround: site leaders submit a number; HR puts it on the spreadsheet;
  the executive level sets the percentage; payroll uploads.<br>
  *"And then all salary increases aren't done at any of the site levels or even myself.
  That's done by pay works or by payroll."* — Their HR Manager
- **Regional scorecard comparisons are maintained by hand** — severity: low-medium —
  workaround: a running Excel outside every system, deliberately not shared with
  employees.<br>
  *"So we just have a running Excel that shows the comparison."* — Their Operations Manager

**Feature requests:**

- **Category averages plus a total average, visible before submit** — underlying need:
  reproduce their weighted model in the form, at the moment of rating, when a review can run
  to 15–20 ratings — priority signal: must-have (it was his one closing ask).
  *"average of each category and a total average."*
- **A summary tab on the employee record** — discipline, certification, attendance flags —
  underlying need: prepare a review from one screen — priority signal: nice-to-have.
- **Check-in notes that can serve as HR's supporting documentation** — underlying need: build
  the disciplinary trail continuously instead of interviewing the employee after each
  incident — priority signal: nice-to-have.
- **One-up manager collaboration on a review** (HR Manager's ask, in place of peer feedback)
  — underlying need: today *"the one-up manager can't see that review unless it's actually
  shared with them"* — priority signal: must-have, from the buyer.

**Pain Points Validated:**

- ✅ Self-rating inflation is real and universal at this account.
- ✅ Managers do not own the pay outcome — the increase is set above them and applied by
  payroll.
- ❌ That managers keep review-ready notes in a system — they do not; only disciplinary
  documents reach Payworks, uploaded as files.
- ❓ Whether a category-average display would actually change how they rate, or only how they
  check their work before submitting.

### Interview — District Manager (several sites, ~12 reviews per cycle), 2026-06-22

**Research goal:** What breaks for a manager carrying a full team across multiple sites?
**Hypotheses tested:** that the self-review changes the manager's rating; that the review
cycle has enforceable deadlines; that managers gather outside input today.

**Jobs-to-be-Done:**
When I run a review cycle across several sites, I want to set the site's goals once, be
reminded of what is outstanding, and have the year's moments already written down, so that
twelve reviews stop being twelve rounds of retyping and remembering.

**Pain points:**

- **The same goal is retyped for every person on a site** — severity: high — workaround:
  type it again per employee, flipping between screens to keep the wording consistent.<br>
  *"I may have four at one site. They all got the same goals, but then when I'm going in,
  I'm typing that goal for every single person. So it takes a long time."* — Their District
  Manager
- **No reminders exist anywhere in the cycle** — severity: high — workaround: the HR Manager
  emails, then managers chase supervisors.<br>
  *"we don't get, like, automated replies saying, hey, you have this to do, make sure it's
  done by this date, kind of thing. So it'd be nice to have some automated reminders."* —
  Their District Manager
- **The year's evidence is on paper and in self-addressed email** — severity: high —
  workaround: a notepad, an agenda, emails to himself — and sometimes none of them.<br>
  *"might be in a notepad somewhere, or I might be sending emails to myself throughout the
  course of the year… I misplaced that notepad somewhere, and I don't know where it is."* —
  Their District Manager
- **Six or seven tabs per review in the incumbent tool** — severity: high — workaround:
  work through them and flip back to check what he wrote.<br>
  *"we've got probably about six or seven tabs where you click on and you're going through
  kind of like this, but it's, it's very cumbersome. It's very time consuming."* — Their
  District Manager
- **No visibility into whether the employee has started** — severity: medium — workaround:
  open QuickBase, where "started" may mean nothing happened.<br>
  *"it'll say the employee started, but starting might mean that they actually opened it,
  but they haven't done anything yet either, right?"* — Their District Manager
- **Six months is longer than his memory of the work** — severity: medium — workaround: he
  wants a quarterly touchpoint; today he reconstructs at mid-year and year-end.<br>
  *"six months is a long time, especially in this business, because we're here quite a bit.
  So, I mean, you tend to forget some things as well, too."* — Their District Manager
- **Goal risk is assessed by digging through back-end systems** — severity: medium —
  workaround: pull financials and health-and-safety trends out of QuickBase by hand.<br>
  *"It's a lot of work in the back end, put it that way, to go back and check all that
  stuff."* — Their District Manager

**Feature requests:**

  rinse-and-repeat typing; he accepts that admin roles need a tweaked variant — priority
  signal: must-have.
- **Automated reminders on both sides of the review** — underlying need: stop the cycle
  running on nagging; he would accept daily nudges — priority signal: must-have.
- **Private manager notes kept through the year** — underlying need: replace the notepad
  with something that cannot be misplaced, visible only to him — priority signal:
  nice-to-have, but he called it *"huge."*
- **Peer / cross-department feedback, entered by the giver** — underlying need: record
  recognition at the moment it happens and let the employee see it — priority signal: wanted
  by him, contested by HR.
- **Quarterly review or quarterly manager update** — underlying need: keep the record fresh
  — priority signal: nice-to-have.
- **Self-review progress visibility** — underlying need: know who to chase — priority
  signal: nice-to-have.
- **Succession/readiness signal on the review** — underlying need: separate the people who
  want the next tier from the people who are happy where they are — priority signal:
  nice-to-have.
- **Per-category rating rather than one overall meets/exceeds** — recorded as part of the
  weighted-scoring request: *"I would think it'd almost be nice to have one for each one,
  and then it gives you whatever the number may be at the end."*

**Pain Points Validated:**

- ✅ Deadlines exist on paper and are not enforced by the system.
- ✅ The self-review does not change his rating — *"I haven't gone back and changed any of my
  views based on what somebody else is putting out"* — though he wants the option.
- ✅ Managers do not set merit percentages — *"that's all the COO. the COO and the Head of
  HR. Yeah, and the VP of Operations sometimes."*

  recognition, not for scoring, when asked directly.

- He wants peer feedback but does **not** want it to shape his own assessment: *"I don't want

### Interview — Regional Operations Manager (drivers, administration, sales), 2026-06-22

**Research goal:** How does a manager of a mixed field-and-office population run reviews,
and what would make him use a system at all?
**Hypotheses tested:** that the 1–5 scale is understood; that goal cascade reaches
non-operational roles; that a desktop tool is sufficient for a manager on a yard.

**Jobs-to-be-Done:**
When something happens on the yard that I will need at review time, I want to capture it in
seconds on my phone, so it lands against that employee instead of into a paper journal only
I can read.

**Pain points:**

- **The 1–5 scale is not understood the same way on both sides** — severity: high —
  workaround: he talks the employee down in the meeting, and it *"more times than not…
  turns into an argument."*<br>
  *"A five is like you're the model that we're, we're creating you as the example kind of
  thing. And there seems to be, that's the biggest disconnect of that. There's not a clear
  understanding, particularly on the self-review side."* — Their Regional Operations Manager
- **Employees rate without commenting, so the manager writes both sides** — severity: high —
  workaround: he writes a justification into every section himself.<br>
  *"It ends up being more work on me in the end… I will go through every section and I'll
  put, this is why you get a three instead of a five."* — Their Regional Operations Manager
- **The rating legend exists but nobody finds it** — severity: high — workaround: none;
  employees skip it.<br>
  *"Realistically, there is, but the way it is right now, it's not the most user-friendly
  interface. So, you kind of have to look for it and know where you're looking for it."* —
  Their Regional Operations Manager
- **Company goals cascade to roles they cannot apply to** — severity: medium-high —
  workaround: he rewrites the goal into something vague and then rates it 3 by default.<br>
  *"What can they do to cut reduce safety incidents when they sit at a desk all day?"* — and
  of the goal he writes instead, *"Which is a vague, really crappy goal in reality."* — Their
  Regional Operations Manager
- **Goal tracking is a personal spreadsheet with no link to the review** — severity: high —
  workaround: maintain it by hand, transcribe at review time.<br>
  *"for tracking goals, it's a completely… it's an Excel sheet that I… I maintain and see
  that is nowhere related to it whatsoever, to the actual performance review document."* —
  Their Regional Operations Manager
- **Everything worth remembering is in a paper journal** — severity: high — workaround: a
  colour-coded notebook with weekly summaries, tabs and folds.<br>
  *"I have a notebook of… Every… almost like a journal."* and, of the same book, *"I kind of
  assign colors to departments."* — Their Regional Operations Manager
- **Deadlines are not real** — severity: medium — workaround: a calendar entry with a daily
  reminder; the cycle is extended every year, and the process changes with each new HR

  *"On the surface, yes. In reality, no."* — Their Regional Operations Manager
- **Recognition given in passing is lost** — severity: medium — workaround: none.<br>
  *"even if it's in an email, hey, X did a good job today, that's probably going in a folder

**Feature requests:**

- **Rating-scale anchors shown at the point of rating** — underlying need: make "what a 5
  means" impossible to miss so the self-rating stops being a negotiation — priority signal:
  must-have. He named the prototype's version as the fix: *"I definitely like the role model
  needs a tangible example that pops up right away."*
- **Mandatory written justification for a rating** — underlying need: stop the manager
  writing both sides; he even proposed a character minimum — priority signal: must-have.
- **Mobile capture** — underlying need: beat the notebook on speed, in the moment —
  priority signal: nice-to-have, but stated as the condition of adoption.
- **A reminder surfaced where he already works** — underlying need: he is in Payworks daily
  approving time cards, so the nudge should live there — priority signal: nice-to-have
  (corroborates the district manager's must-have).
- **Private vs. shareable notes** — underlying need: some notes are for his level only —
  priority signal: nice-to-have (corroborates the district manager).
- **Cross-department feedback for dual-reporting staff** — underlying need: he holds
  feedback on people who do not report to him and it is *"not currently acknowledged
  anywhere"* — priority signal: wanted, contested by HR.

**Pain Points Validated:**

- ✅ Self-rating inflation, and specifically that the arguments cluster on 5s.
- ✅ The evidence problem — his is the most extreme case in the session (all paper).

- ❌ That a desktop-only tool would be used for in-the-moment capture — he was explicit it
  would not.
- ❓ What happens to a review after submission — he genuinely does not know, and said so.
