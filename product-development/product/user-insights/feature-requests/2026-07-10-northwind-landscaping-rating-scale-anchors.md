# [Performance Management] Rating-scale definitions anchored per question and per job

Northwind Landscaping's **site supervisor** — the first salaried, reviewed level in their
field org — ran a review round for his crew on paper in 2026 and named scale calibration as
the single hardest part of it. He used a 1–5 scale and likes 1–5; what failed was that the
scale carried only generic top-of-page labels off a downloaded template, so his crew could
not tell what a 3 was supposed to mean for their actual job. The result was systematically
depressed self-ratings, which he then had to talk each person out of, one at a time.

The need is a scale whose points are **defined in the employee's own job language, next to
the question being scored**, so that a self-assessment is usable evidence rather than a
proxy for how modest someone is feeling. This is the input side of the score gap the whole
review conversation is built on: he scores, they score, and he works the difference.

*"Honestly, finding the, finding the, the scale was hard. Like, because I would say rate
yourself out of, they don't know."* — Their Site Supervisor

*"how… how hard to rate themselves, right? So that was honestly the most challenging, was a
lot of them were just too hard on themselves, or they were just playing it safe, rating
themselves low, and then, hey, I go up higher, and then I bring them up. So there was a
couple of that, right?"* — Their Site Supervisor

*"Uh, it did have, like, uh, like, at the top, like, excellent, exceeds, um, satisfactory,
needs improvement, and I can't remember the last one, but yeah."* — Their Site Supervisor
(on what the downloaded form gave him)

*"And like I said, I kind of just found it off of the internet. It wasn't specified
specifically for each job, so there was a couple of things where I had to kind of explain."*
— Their Site Supervisor

**Signal strength — read this before ranking.** This record is derived from a stated pain,
not from an explicit ask. He never asked for anchored scales by name; he described the
problem in detail and endorsed the direction — *"what you guys are doing is amazing, so that
would be a lot, lot better."* One voice, one account, unprompted, and named by him as *the
most challenging* part of running reviews. Treat the pain as strong evidence and the
mechanism as our proposal, not his.

**What they do today:** he explains the scoring verbally, employee by employee, then revises
individual scores upward in the meeting when someone has under-rated themselves.

## Draft ticket

**Objective:** Let a review question carry rating-point definitions in the language of the
job being reviewed, visible to both the employee self-assessing and the manager scoring, so
self-ratings arrive calibrated instead of conservative.

**Acceptance criteria seed:**
- Each rating point on a question's scale can carry its own short definition, authored
  alongside the question rather than as a single scale legend for the whole form.
- Definitions are visible at the point of scoring, on both the employee self-assessment and
  the manager review — not only on a help page or a form header.
- Definitions travel with the question when a form is copied or varied for another
  site/department (see the site-level template request filed the same day).
- The 1–5 scale itself is preserved: the ask is about defining the points, not replacing the
  scale. He used 1–5 by choice and did not object to it.
- The employee's and the manager's scores remain separately visible after submission, since
  the score gap is what the review conversation is built on.
