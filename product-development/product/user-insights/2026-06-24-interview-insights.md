## Top Pain Points (ranked)

1. **Nothing tracks performance between review cycles, so evidence lives in a personal
   spreadsheet** — 1 of 1 interviews — *"I kind of kept track just on a spreadsheet on my
   computer, anything that we needed to work on, um, anything they were excelling in." —
   Their Site Operations Manager* — impact: the year's evidence sits outside the review
   system on one manager's machine, unreadable by HR and unavailable at review time to
   anyone but him — workaround: a spreadsheet on his own computer; asked directly whether
   anything *else* was tracked on the side, he answered *"Uh, nope."*

2. **An annual cycle is too coarse to keep up with performance** — 1 of 1 — *"a quarterly
   review instead of the yearly one, because it'd be a lot easier… I know we do the
   six-month, but it might be easier if it's every quarter, just to kind of keep up with the
   performance." — Their Site Operations Manager* — impact: issues surface late; he wants
   anything glaring *"documented, brought to attention on their performance"* nearer to when
   it happens — workaround: continuous informal contact (twice-daily check-ins) that leaves
   no record.

3. **Review workload is doubling against no spare time** — 1 of 1 — *"Uh, it was 2 last
   cycle, but this one, it will be 4." — Their Site Operations Manager*, followed by *"Yeah,
   which is, I'm finding a hard time finding spare time right now."* — impact: per-review
   effort is the constraint on this tier, and it is about to be tested at 2× — workaround:
   none offered.

4. **Assignments to the team are issued by email and go untracked** — 1 of 1 — *"I'll set,
   like, if we need, uh, certain observations done on drivers, I'll just kind of… it'll be
   quick email, but it'd be nice with some system like this that I can track it a little
   better than just sending emails" — Their Site Operations Manager* — impact: manager-set
   work has no completion trail and cannot inform the review — workaround: email.

5. **Daily check-ins happen and are never captured** — 1 of 1 — *"Um, I try to do it daily,
   um, stuff like that, just little things" — Their Site Operations Manager* — impact: the
   richest, most frequent performance signal at this tier is entirely verbal; asked whether
   he collects feedback from his reports today, he answered *"I don't."* — workaround: none;
   the conversation happens and evaporates.

**Explicitly weak or absent, recorded as such:** he named no pain with the review software
itself. Asked what was not working, his answer was that *"it wasn't too complicated to
operate"*; asked what he could not live without, *"Uh, I honestly didn't
notice really anything. Like I said, first time using it, I was just getting used to that
platform."* He is a first-cycle reviewer with no accumulated grievance — not a satisfied
one. Note also that "the system" here is their incumbent review tool, not Payworks; he never
named it.

## Interviews

## Interview — Site Operations Manager, multi-site grounds maintenance / seasonal operations (2026-06-24)

**Research goal:** learn how a front-line people-manager actually runs performance
management — how ratings get decided, what is tracked between cycles, what happens outside
the product — and then get his hands on the Performance Management prototype (home, review
writing, goals, check-ins, feedback, employee profile) to see what fits.

**Hypotheses tested:**
- Managers see the employee's self-review before writing their own → **disproved for this
  account's current system**
- The self-review shifts the manager's ratings → **disproved for this manager, this cycle**
- Managers keep evidence somewhere outside the review system → **confirmed**
- Structured check-ins and colleague feedback fit an existing behaviour at this tier →
  **partly — the check-in behaviour exists daily; the feedback-collection behaviour does not**

**Jobs-to-be-Done:**
When I have supervisors reporting to me whose performance I see constantly but record
nowhere, I want the small things I notice through the year to be captured where the review
already lives, so I can write a fair review quickly without reconstructing the year from my
own spreadsheet.

**Pain points:** (severity + current workaround for each)

- **No between-cycle record of performance** — severity: high — workaround: a personal
  spreadsheet on his own computer, holding *"anything that we needed to work on"* and
  *"anything they were excelling in"*<br>
  *"I kind of kept track just on a spreadsheet on my computer, anything that we needed to work on, um, anything they were excelling in." — Their Site Operations Manager*

- **Annual cadence too coarse; wants quarterly** — severity: medium — workaround: constant
  informal contact that leaves no documentation<br>
  *"a quarterly review instead of the yearly one, because it'd be a lot easier… I know we do the six-month, but it might be easier if it's every quarter, just to kind of keep up with the performance." — Their Site Operations Manager*

- **Review load doubling with no spare time** — severity: medium — workaround: none named<br>
  *"Yeah, which is, I'm finding a hard time finding spare time right now." — Their Site Operations Manager*

- **First-cycle reviewer with no scaffolding for how to rate** — severity: medium —
  workaround: rate against the job description, plus what he has seen through the year<br>
  *"Um, so I'm still fairly new. This, uh, this past year for the reviews was my first time doing them. Um, so it was, uh, just a bit of a learning curve for me, just how to actually rate." — Their Site Operations Manager*<br>
  *"Um, so usually I just go over how they've been performing over the year, um, and make sure they're doing, kind of, their job description, and then I just kind of base… I based it off of that this year." — Their Site Operations Manager*

- **Assignments issued by email, never tracked** — severity: medium — workaround: *"quick
  email"*<br>
  *"I'll set, like, if we need, uh, certain observations done on drivers, I'll just kind of… it'll be quick email, but it'd be nice with some system like this that I can track it a little better than just sending emails" — Their Site Operations Manager*

- **Daily check-ins uncaptured; no feedback collected from reports** — severity: medium —
  workaround: verbal only, twice a day<br>
  *"Uh, usually in the morning, once the rollout's complete, we'll just kinda sit in the office and just run down the day, how the day's looking, check in, make sure if they have any issues, or if there's anything I need them… any issues I have that I need them to take care of." — Their Site Operations Manager*<br>
  Asked whether he collects feedback from his reports today: *"I don't." — Their Site Operations Manager*

**How the current cycle actually runs (his account of it):**
- Two cycles — mid-year and annual; employee self-review plus manager review, then a
  discussion.
- **His review is submitted first.** *"Uh, no, I got it after I had done it. Once I had put
  the review in, it sent the notification through for them to do their own self-review." —
  Their Site Operations Manager*
- **The self-review does not move his ratings.** *"this year I didn't change anything, any
  sort of, like, oh, I thought I was doing better at this, but it stayed how I originally put
  it." — Their Site Operations Manager* — his reasoning was that *"They… they aren't
  seeing some of their faults"*.
- **No disputes last cycle.** *"So there was no real discrepancies between how they felt they
  performed and how I thought they performed. So it was easy."* He attributes this to
  proximity: *"Yeah, I work out on-site with them, so at least twice an hour, they're in my
  office, or I'm down in their office, we're talking on the phone, so it's constant
  communication" — Their Site Operations Manager*.
- **Inputs to a rating:** the job description, performance over the year, plus incidents —
  *"incidents would count into it if we've had, uh, any… for… in the aspect of supervisor, if
  there's been complaints about them"* and *"any constructive criticism that's being brought
  forth by the drivers towards them"*.
- **Goals:** employees set their own during the self-review and they are compared year over
  year.

**Prototype reactions** (screens are Payworks mock data — reactions recorded, screen values not):

- **Home / "needs you" reminders — strong positive.** *"the… the needs you section, um, just
  kind of little reminders about, like, the one-on-ones overdue, stuff like that's very handy
  to have." — Their Site Operations Manager*, and *"Trying to schedule it in, and then the
  reminder, having it right there is nice."* Asked whether anything he expected was missing:
  *"Uh, nope."*
- **Self-review visible while writing the manager review — positive.** *"How they thought
  they did and how I thought they did is on there." — Their Site Operations Manager* — the
  value he named was seeing the size of the gap before committing.
- **Goals panel beside the review — positive, and a stated time saving.** *"Uh… yeah, it
  would help, so at least I can compare what they had set for their goals, or what they… what
  was set for them, and I… it's right there, I don't have to go back and look, so it's, uh…
  it seems… would make the process a lot quicker." — Their Site Operations Manager*
- **Feedback-received panel — positive, same reason.** *"Yeah, so it kind of works, so then at
  least I know what the last feedback was on their goals, and then I can compare it to what
  I'm doing, and it's at a glance, so I don't have to, again, jump back and forth between
  different screens." — Their Site Operations Manager*
- **Ratings scale — familiar, and the layout beats the incumbent.** *"Yeah, it's similar to
  what we're doing now, the one through five rating."* and *"just having everything on one
  page, it's not multiple pages, um, to go through. The setup of it's just a lot, uh… it's a
  nice, simpler setup than to what we're currently using." — Their Site Operations Manager*
- **Overall score — no signal.** Asked directly whether an overall score would be useful and
  whether anything was missing from the page, he answered *"Um, now does it… uh, no,
  everything's, uh, it's right there. Um, yeah, no, everything looks good to me."* That is a
  non-answer, not an endorsement; the question is still open at this tier.
- **Check-ins — would use, with a boundary.** *"Yeah, I would definitely use it. For small
  things, probably not use it. If there's a small ongoing issue, then I would probably use it
  and use it that way for, yeah, more ongoing issues." — Their Site Operations Manager*, and
  as review input: *"I would definitely use it as a good tool just to go back for when I am
  doing the reviews."* He linked it back to cadence himself: *"if it is a quarterly review,
  the check-ins won't be — it won't be as long of a list."*
- **Check-ins — he asked the configuration question unprompted.** *"Now, do they see this as
  well for the check-ins? Can they add anything to it, or is it just, um… would it just be,
  like, me seeing it?" — Their Site Operations Manager* — visibility and employee
  contribution are the first thing he wanted to know about the surface.
- **Feedback module — positive on tracking, thin on why he does not do it today.** *"then
  they can answer right away, see what their feelings are on it. Um, and then at least it's
  tracked, and then you can go back and…" — Their Site Operations Manager* ("Refer to it
  later on."). He would use praise and constructive notes both — *"Uh, no, I would use… I
  would use both."* Probed on what stops him collecting feedback today, he declined: *"Yeah,
  the feedback, I can't really answer that one."* — recorded as unanswered, roughly a
  45-second exchange, no requirement in it.
- **Feedback visibility — a comprehension miss worth fixing.** Asked where his notes would
  land across public / manager-only / private, he said *"Um, it'd probably be 50-50"* — and
  then described private as something other than private: *"Well, for the private, I believe
  it would just be between you and the the person that it's directed to just directly. And
  then the public one would be anyone that has access to that person's profile on here." —
  Their Site Operations Manager*
- **Employee profile page — positive.** *"Very simple to navigate, everything's there. The
  reminders for the next one-on-one is nice, too."* and *"Yeah, this would be a very handy
  tool." — Their Site Operations Manager*
- **Overall + learning curve.** *"It's nice. It's a nice, simple platform to navigate. It's
  not clicking numerous pages over just to get to one."* and *"Yeah, no, I don't think it
  wouldn't be too much of a learning curve, just something new to learn." — Their Site
  Operations Manager*

**Surprises:**

- **He was interviewed from his truck, and never turned that into a product ask.** His HR
  Manager introduced it — *"I think the Site Operations Manager's in his truck today"* — and
  he apologised for it himself: *"No, sorry that I'm not at my desk doing this properly. I do
  apologize." — Their Site Operations Manager*. He raised mobile access at no point in the
  session, and nobody asked him about it. Recorded here as context about **where this persona
  is when we need him**, not as a requirement: he is a field manager who took a research call
  from a vehicle, and separately described having an office on site (*"they're in my office,
  or I'm down in their office"*). He is not desk-less; he was simply not at a desk that day.
  The absence of a mobile ask from a manager sitting in a truck is itself the finding worth
  probing later — it is a gap in our questioning, not evidence that mobile does not matter.

- **The sequencing assumption was wrong, and he corrected it.** The interviewer asked whether
  he receives the self-review before submitting his own; the real order is the reverse, and
  the self-review is only requested once his review is in.

- **The best-received screens were the reminders and the panels, not the review form.** What
  he praised first on every screen was not having to jump between windows — the goals panel,
  the feedback panel, the overdue-one-on-one reminder. The consolidation argument at this tier
  is about *screens*, not about the review artefact.

- **He is the corpus's clearest low-grievance respondent.** He reported no problem with the
  current review software, no favourite feature, and no missing feature on any prototype
  screen. That is a first-cycle reviewer with nothing accumulated to complain about — treat
  his enthusiasm and his indifference as equally weak signals, and weight the two things he
  volunteered unprompted (quarterly cadence, email-tracked assignments) far above the things
  he agreed with.

- **The visibility model did not land.** His reading of "private" was the opposite of a
  private note. One respondent, one screen — but it is a cheap thing to test again before it
  is specified.
