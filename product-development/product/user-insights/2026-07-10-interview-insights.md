## Executive Summary

- **The strongest incumbent-displacement signal in the Northwind set is that there was no
  incumbent to displace at his level.** He ran a full review round for his crew off his own
  initiative, on printed sheets, from a template he found on the internet and edited in
  Microsoft Word — *"I kind of went a little rogue on it. There wasn't…"* / *"a system in
  place that I was aware of, so I kind of just wanted to do it."* The output went to his
  manager as a paper stack and stopped there:
  *"There was nothing for me in that in that sense."*
- **The 1–5 scale is his hardest problem, and it is a definition problem, not a mechanic
  problem.** He used 1–5 and liked it; what failed was that his crew could not calibrate
  against a generic, off-the-internet scale — *"a lot of them were just too hard on
  themselves, or they were just playing it safe, rating themselves low"*
- **He wants a flat average, explicitly — which puts him against Northwind's operations
  director**, who wants weighted category scoring (recorded on the initiative page from a
  record not yet filed). His reasoning is that safety and craft are not rankable against
  each other: *"I think they're all important in their own way"*. Reported as a divergence,
  not averaged.
- **Goals, as the prototype frames them, do not fit a seasonal field crew — he reframed the
  screen into an employee-declared "expression of interest" register** for training and
  committee seats, which is an employee-self-service capability, not a target-setting one.
- **Prototype reception: positive on reviews, blocked on check-ins, and legibility is a real
  gate.** He could not read the screen at default size (*"it's really small, and I have
  glasses"*) and needed the reviews-vs-check-ins distinction explained before Check-ins made
  any sense to him.

## Top Pain Points (ranked)

1. **No system of record for reviews at his level — the round he ran exists only on paper.**
   1 of 1 interviews — *"after I did the review and I had the stacks, I forward everything
   to my manager, just to kind of say, this is what I did. But as far as, like, an actual,
   uh, uploading it to a system and having it there on file for future."* / *"There was
   nothing for me in that in that sense." — Their Site Supervisor* — impact: a completed
   review cycle for a whole crew produced zero durable, retrievable record; nothing is on
   the employee file, nothing is comparable next cycle — workaround: hand the paper stack to
   his manager.

2. **Employees cannot calibrate against a generic rating scale.** 1 of 1 — *"Honestly,
   finding the, finding the, the scale was hard. Like, because I would say rate yourself out
   of, they don't know."* / *"how… how hard to rate themselves, right? So that was honestly
   the most challenging, was a lot of them were just too hard on themselves, or they were
   just playing it safe, rating themselves low, and then, hey, I go up higher, and then I
   bring them up." — Their Site Supervisor* — impact: self-ratings are systematically
   depressed, so the score gap he uses to open the conversation is noise as often as signal —
   workaround: he explained the scoring verbally, employee by employee, and revised
   individual scores upward in the meeting.

3. **The template did not fit the job.** 1 of 1 — *"And like I said, I kind of just found it
   off of the internet. It wasn't specified specifically for each job, so there was a couple
   of things where I had to kind of explain." — Their Site Supervisor* — impact: every review
   conversation carried an explain-the-form tax before it could carry content; sites differ
   operationally (depot vs no depot, soil vs no soil) so one company-wide form under-fits by
   construction — workaround: he edited a downloaded Word template toward his own job
   descriptions, then explained the rest out loud.

4. **The prototype is too dense to read on his hardware.** 1 of 1 — *"like, ship the… um,
   sorry, it's really small, and I have glasses, so… pardon if I can't read it." — Their Site
   Supervisor* — impact: he could not begin the walkthrough until he zoomed the browser;
   for a supervisor who is not a desk worker this is an adoption gate, not a nitpick —
   workaround: Ctrl-+ browser zoom, prompted by the moderator.

5. **Check-ins, as presented, did not communicate their own purpose.** 1 of 1 — *"Okay, I'm
   a little confused, like…"* / *"Check in. Yeah, I don't really get this part, like,
   what…" — Their Site Supervisor* — impact: the screen needed a verbal explanation from the
   PM before it made sense, and even once understood he does not run scheduled sit-downs —
   *"The day just flows, and when, uh, when instances pop up, we deal with them, like, as
   they come, kind of deal."* — workaround: none; the behaviour the screen assumes does not
   exist at his site.

6. **Continuous performance notes exist only in his memory.** 1 of 1 — *"I mean, my memory,
   just the way things go, I'm kind of good at just remembering that stuff and keeping
   details like that organized, but I mean, if it was just, like, a blanket program, I would
   say you'd want to have, like, little notes and stuff like that that you could, uh, add to
   it throughout the year kind of deal." — Their Site Supervisor* — impact: severity is
   **low for him and high for the rollout** — he is explicit that a program shipped to
   everyone needs the notes he personally does not need — workaround: he works very closely
   with his crew and recalls incidents unaided.

## Interviews

### Interview — Site Supervisor, multi-site grounds maintenance / seasonal operations (2026-07-10)

**Research goal:** how a front-line field supervisor actually runs performance reviews and
tracks his crew through the year, and what he does when he meets the Performance Management
prototype for the first time.

**Hypotheses tested:** the prototype's implicit bets, put to him in-session — not a
pre-registered list.
- A scored review with a computed average at the bottom is useful to a field manager.
- Category weighting is wanted.
- A recurring, lightweight check-in surface fits a manager's week.
- Goals mean measurable targets.
- Peer-to-peer feedback is wanted at the front line.
- Review-form configuration belongs at the company/HR level.

**Jobs-to-be-Done:**
When I see people on my crew tailing off, or new employees reading my coaching as
punishment, I want to sit each of them down with a scored, two-sided record of how they are
doing, so I can raise morale and tell them where they stand before it becomes trouble.

Second job: when I need someone for first aid, forklift, or the health-and-safety
committee, I want to look up who has already said they are interested, so I can stop walking
the yard asking.

**Pain points:** (severity + current workaround for each)
- **No system of record — the completed round lives on paper only** — severity: high —
  workaround: paper stack forwarded to his manager, nothing on file.<br>
  *"Uh, so I kind of… I kind of went a little rogue on it. There wasn't…"* / *"a system in
  place that I was aware of, so I kind of just wanted to do it." — Their Site Supervisor*

**Feature requests:** (underlying need, not just the ask)
- **Rating-scale definitions anchored per question and per job** — underlying need: crews
  that can self-rate honestly without a supervisor calibrating them one at a time; today the
  labels are the generic *"excellent, exceeds, um, satisfactory, needs improvement"* off a
  downloaded form — priority signal: must-have. *Derived from his stated hardest problem, not
  from an explicit ask; what he actually said about our direction was* **"what you guys are
  doing is amazing, so that would be a lot, lot better."**
- **Site- / manager-level configurable review templates** — underlying need: a form that
  matches what a given yard actually does, maintained by someone on site — *"I think

- **Employee expression-of-interest register** — underlying need: a standing, employee-owned

- **Clickable overdue counters on the manager home** — underlying need: one click from "two
  reviews overdue" into the reviews themselves — priority signal: nice-to-have.

**Pain Points Validated:**
- ✅ A computed average at the bottom of a review is wanted by a field manager — *"I think a
  good average is nice, yeah. I, I just, I'm a visual guy, so I like seeing, like, numbers
  like that."*
- ✅ Self-assessment before the manager conversation is already his own invented practice —
  he scored his crew, had them score themselves, then worked the gap: *"I would do like my
  score one to five, their score one to five, and then I would do the adding and I would go,
  okay, we're about four points off here, four points off here."*
- ✅ The review conversation is a morale instrument, not only an evaluation one — *"I saw a
  couple people were kind of tailing off, and I just wanted to have that heart-to-heart to
  kind of bring… bring the morale back, right?"*
- ❌ Category weighting is wanted — he rejected it for a flat average: *"I think they're all
  important in their own way, so personally, I would say just an average. So, yeah, an
  average down the columns. I wouldn't say anything's more"*.
- ❌ A recurring check-in surface fits his week — it does not: *"The day just flows, and
  when, uh, when instances pop up, we deal with them, like, as they come, kind of deal."*
- ❌ Goals mean measurable targets — for seasonal field work he ruled that out: *"How many
  pallets have you wrapped today, when some days are slower, some… winter, summer, it varies
  so much?"*
- ❌ Review-template configuration belongs to HR — he put it with managers, *"maybe with the
  help of the supervisor, since we're kind of on hands a little more"*.
- ❓ Peer-to-peer feedback at the front line — **thin, and not answered as asked.** Vanessa
  Lee asked directly whether employees should give each other feedback; he answered about the
  value of the face-to-face manager conversation instead — *"I think bringing them in, having
  that communication, that face-to-face interaction is really important."* On a praise
  feature he agreed it would help morale, framed as breaking the office's punishment
  association: *"you hear the old thing, like, the only time I'm brought in the office is
  when I'm getting rid up and stuff"*. That is roughly a 90-second exchange and contains no
  requirement. Do not count it as support for peer feedback either way.
- ❓ Whether his self-started round was sanctioned. He forwarded it to his manager after the
  fact; nobody told him to run it. The account page records that the company's formal cycle
  covers salaried and administrative staff only, which would put his crew outside it —
  (inferred, across records; not stated in this interview).

**Surprises:**
