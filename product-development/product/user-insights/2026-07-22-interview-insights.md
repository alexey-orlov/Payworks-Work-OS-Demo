Margaret Foster (research, facilitator) and Michelle Tremblay. Customer side: their HR Lead

## Executive Summary

- **Manager mobile is the named adoption gate for this account, and it is confirmed in her own words twice over.** Today's app is employee-scoped — *"Right now, app is strictly for recording times, or time of requests, or whatever, so managers cannot do their stuff. Because a lot of our managers are constantly on the go."* Asked directly whether mobile-friendly-for-manager would encourage adoption, she answered *"Yeah, yeah, a lot, a lot."* The single capability she would put on the phone first is note capture straight after a conversation; formal discipline she wants kept off it.
- **Percentages do not work for this account; dollars per hour do.** *"I'm not sure what you mean, it's very hard to deal with percentages"* and *"Our company, we use dollar value, like, 100%, so it would be nice to have an option for dollar value."* The prototype's merit-increase field offered percentage only — a concrete gap, not a preference.
- **HR approval and compensation approval are two different acts, and conflating them is the biggest workflow finding of the session.** *"What is important for me to emphasize is that if I, as an HR, click approve and share, it doesn't mean that this is approved."* The review can be shared with the employee; the wage change goes to the CEO, individually — *"one at a time, because it's a serious decision about wage increase"* — and today that hop happens by email. She wants the recommendation to *"trigger… some kind of, task for the CEO"*.
- **The strongest unprompted enthusiasm was for the role profile with key competencies** — *"I would love to have it in the platform! It's, like, so cool!"* — because it makes manager and employee agree on what is being evaluated before the rating starts. Nothing like it exists in Payworks today: *"we don't use anything, like, job descriptions in paywalks yet"*
- **Adoption risk in this session is ramp and avoidance, not density.** She expects managers to need time — *"It will take some time for them to get used to this"* — and reports existing avoidance of the Discipline tab. She raised **no** complaint about screen density in this session; the density concern recorded elsewhere in the corpus is not hers and is not imported here.

## Top Pain Points (ranked)

1. **Managers cannot act in Payworks away from a desk, so performance work never gets recorded at the moment it happens** — 1 of 1 interviews (this account's named adoption gate) — *"Because a lot of our managers are constantly on the go."* — impact: the whole check-in/notes surface degrades to email and memory — workaround: managers email HR after a conversation and HR re-keys it — *"Saying that I just had a conversation with such and such employee, and that's what we agreed, and then I would go in pay works."*
2. **Every conversation record is hand-carried into three places** — 1 of 1 — *"I would usually save this email as PDF, attached to employee's file, make it visible for employee, but then I would also create a record in Payworks on the Discipline tab."* — impact: pure re-keying, and it is HR's time, not the manager's — workaround: the manual chain itself; notes otherwise live in a Word performance-review form — *"we just put all, all the notes in, in a Word? In performance review form"* — and, in her own assessment of how well that works, *"Not well."*
3. **The wage-increase hop from review to CEO to payroll runs entirely on email** — 1 of 1 — *"That is what's happening right now, but by mail."* — impact: no audit trail on the approval, no task for the approver, HR chases — workaround: manager emails the CEO with the review attached; once approved, HR actions it into the next pay period. Staged increases live in an unmanaged spreadsheet: *"I would put it in my magical spreadsheet, so we remember that it's a plan."*
4. **Manager avoidance of the existing HR surfaces is already real** — 1 of 1 — *"Some managers even… they just don't dare to use the paperwork, because there is a, discipline"* — impact: any new manager-facing surface inherits this resistance — workaround: HR does it for them.
5. **A rating with no comment box forces the explanation somewhere else** — 1 of 1 — *"it's always, like, in any reviews, if I give, especially negative"* the context matters — impact: ratings arrive unexplained and the acknowledgement conversation gets harder — workaround: explanation goes in the Word form's free-text or is spoken and lost.
6. **The manager could not see the employee's self-assessment while rating** — 1 of 1, prototype gap — *"Of course, this is the main point of self-assessment"* — impact: defeats the purpose of collecting it — workaround today: managers hand out the form, collect it, *"and then they compare the results"* by eye.
7. **Wayfinding inside the prototype** — she asked how to go back twice, from two different screens — *"I don't know how to go back besides, like, just."* — impact: low-severity usability, but repeated — workaround: n/a (prototype).

## Interview — HR Lead and HR Coordinator, design-partner manufacturer (2026-07-22)

**Research goal:** Watch an HR buyer move through the Performance Management prototype's
employee and manager surfaces unaided, and learn where her real process diverges from what
the prototype assumes — especially around approval, compensation and the manager's day.

**Hypotheses tested:**
- HR is an approval step in the review workflow.
- A merit recommendation attached to a review is useful to a manager as designed.
- Check-ins and peer feedback read as self-explanatory on the employee surface.

**Jobs-to-be-Done:**
When a manager finishes a conversation with an employee away from a desk, I want the
agreement recorded against that employee immediately and visibly, so I can stop being the
person who re-types other people's conversations into three systems.

When a manager recommends a wage change, I want it to reach the CEO as a decision he can
make on its own, so the raise does not live in my inbox and my spreadsheet.

**Pain points:** (severity + current workaround for each)

- **Managers have no Payworks surface away from the desk** — severity: high — workaround: manager emails HR, HR re-keys<br>
  *"Because you mentioned that there will be an app for managers. Right now, app is strictly for recording times, or time of requests, or whatever, so managers cannot do their stuff. Because a lot of our managers are constantly on the go." — Their HR Lead*
- **Conversation records are hand-carried into the file, the PDF and the Discipline tab** — severity: high — workaround: the manual chain<br>
  *"But I would usually save this email as PDF, attached to employee's file, make it visible for employee, but then I would also create a record in Payworks on the Discipline tab." — Their HR Lead*
- **Notes and goals live in Word forms and scattered files** — severity: high — workaround: none that works<br>
  *"it's all, like, going everywhere. We're creating some files, we, like…" — Their HR Lead / HR Coordinator*<br>
  Asked how that is working: *"Not well. I can, I cannot even imagine managers who are managing, like, 5, 6, 7 employees." — Their HR Lead*
- **The raise chain is email, and the approval is invisible to the system** — severity: high — workaround: email to the CEO, cc HR; staged increases tracked in a personal spreadsheet<br>
  *"That is what's happening right now, but by mail." — Their HR Lead*
- **Managers already avoid the existing HR surface** — severity: medium — workaround: HR absorbs the work<br>
  *"Some managers even… they just don't dare to use the paperwork, because there is a, discipline" — Their HR Lead*
- **Percentage-only merit input** — severity: medium (blocking for their process) — workaround: they would not use the field<br>
  *"I'm not sure what you mean, it's very hard to deal with percentages." — Their HR Lead*
- **A scheduled check-in that does not say what it is about** — severity: low-medium — workaround: ask the manager<br>
  *"I just know that in 8 days, I will have some kind of check-in, but what check-in? What if I don't remember what is this check-in about?" — Their HR Lead / HR Coordinator*<br>
  (The counter and date on that card are prototype mock values.)
- **No back-navigation out of the role profile** — severity: low — workaround: the left-hand menu, once Margaret pointed at it<br>
  *"now I came here and I don't know how to go back besides, like, just." — Their HR Lead / HR Coordinator*

**Feature requests:** (underlying need, not just the ask)

- **Manager capability on the app, note capture first** — underlying need: record the agreement at the moment it is made, with a date, visible to both sides — priority signal: must-have. *"Take some notes, right away on the phone"*; and the boundary she drew herself — serious disciplinary wording *"usually go through me"* and stays off mobile.
- **Dollar-value merit increase** — underlying need: their compensation is negotiated per hour in dollars — priority signal: must-have.
- **Compensation approval as a routed task to the CEO** — underlying need: an approver who receives a decision rather than an email — priority signal: must-have.
- **Manager sees the employee's self-rating while rating** — underlying need: the comparison is the mechanism — priority signal: must-have.
- **Comment field on every rating** — underlying need: a rating that can be explained and discussed — priority signal: must-have.
- **Role profiles with key competencies in the platform** — underlying need: manager and employee agreeing on what is evaluated before it is evaluated — priority signal: must-have.
- **Recognition capture with a date; constructive feedback routed to HR first** — underlying need: keep customer thank-yous structured instead of PDF'd into a file, and keep concerns from landing on a manager unfiltered — priority signal: nice-to-have.
- **Confidential manager notes with per-item employee visibility** — underlying need: document the pattern before it becomes an action — priority signal: nice-to-have.
- **Wage-increase history at the recommendation point** — underlying need: stop a manager recommending an increase for someone who just had one — priority signal: nice-to-have; she volunteered that it *"may not necessarily pop up in here right away"*

**Pain Points Validated:**
- ✅ **HR is NOT an approver of the review itself.** Hypothesis disproved as stated and replaced with something sharper: *"as HR, I don't need to approve anything. It's the manager's things to review the employee"* — but she does want a review-quality gate she can use, returning the review to the manager with comments: *"if the manager rated something as 1 or 2, but employee thinks they are super great, then it requires maybe some elaboration and some actions."*
- ❌ **A merit recommendation as designed is usable.** Disproved on two counts — percentage-only input, and the recommendation being recorded in a place she thinks it should not exist until approved: *"But it might not happen. CEO may not approve it, so why even put this in there?"*
- ❌ **The check-in card is self-explanatory.** Disproved — she could name what it *might* mean (regular one-on-ones, an improvement plan, a new-role cadence, a follow-up on agreed actions) but not what *this* one was. Once she found the check-in history and shared notes, her verdict flipped: *"this is great. I love that they can check in."*
- ✅ **Peer/recognition feedback is wanted, with conditions.** *"Request feedback from someone. This is, like, awesome!"* — conditions being open visibility for positive, HR routing for constructive, and a date on every entry.
- ❓ **Whether "Promotion" belongs on the recommendation at all** — she read it as ambiguous against merit increase (Margaret conceded *"I think you found a bug in our prototype"*) and then argued it is unnecessary at their size. Unclear whether that generalises or is a small-company artifact.

**Surprises:**

- **She corrected the shape of the workflow rather than the screens.** The most valuable output of the session was not a UI note but a model: manager owns the rating, HR owns quality and the share, the CEO owns the money, and those three are not one approval.
- **She wants the compensation recommendation to not be persisted where it could leak before approval**, which cuts against the natural design of attaching it to the review — *"this part should be completely separate. It shouldn't… employees shouldn't see it."*
- **The payroll tail is longer than the review.** The employee is told officially only after the money has actually moved — *"after the pay period finalized, where the three is executed, we send, through Adobe, assign a letter to the employee, saying, this is to confirm that your hourly wage"*, and the letter's own wording, in the next breath: *"Was increased effective such and such date."* A staged increase means the letter comes twice, and *"the official letter will only say when it's Done deal."*
- **She already praised the equivalent Payworks pattern she wants copied twice** — the current wage / new wage preview when an amount is entered (*"It's actually working very, very good right now"*), and the confidential comment on a time-off request, which she offered unprompted as the model for confidential manager notes.
- **No density complaint.** Given that other accounts in this corpus flagged the prototype as busy, it is worth recording that she did not — her nearest remark, *"a lot of things catches my eyes"*, was curiosity, and her adoption worry was manager ramp time, not the interface.
- **Credentials surfaced again in passing** — she expects a role profile to carry *"credentials"* alongside responsibilities and competencies. One sentence, not a thread in this session, but it is the same problem her account is known for.
