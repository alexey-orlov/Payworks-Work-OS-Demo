## Strategic Themes

**Company priorities** — *"Compete and win across Canada — delivering value, modernizing, and scaling with efficiency"*:

1. **Compete and win across Canada** — the arena is correctness for Canadian businesses and their accountants, concentrated first in Ontario and Québec, "where the compliance moat has the most bite"
2. **Deliver more value for every client** — the wallet-share half of the portfolio; multi-module adoption is its measure
3. **Modern, competitive platform** — "Envied for our tech"; where AI investment concentrates
4. **Operational efficiency at scale** — including a channel that "lowers CAC as it grows"

**The product bet running through all four:** *"Product and channel are one coupled bet, not two."* Aurora is the AI-native wedge and the R&D lab whose proven AI flows upmarket into the core; accountants and bookkeepers are the distribution mechanism. Neither is resourced without the other.

**Corporate strategic initiatives:** Sales & market expansion — GTM scaling · Sales & market expansion — CaPDB · Better price-to-value fit — Pricing & packaging · Envied for our tech — Tech modernization · Service excellence — Customer support optimization

## Explicitly Not Doing

The four strategic refusals, stated as *"What we will not do"*:

- **Compete on general-purpose HR feature count** — the differentiator is correctness on Canadian compliance, not parity with general-purpose HR suites. Would change our mind: evidence that feature-count losses, not compliance losses, drive the 39% HR win-loss gap
- **Chase enterprise upmarket** — the base is Canadian SMB; 93% of accounts have under 100 employees
- **Expand outside Canada** — the moat *is* the 13-jurisdiction compliance depth; it does not travel
- **Position AI as autonomous** — product-level bound: the intelligence layer does not make decisions, does not act without user confirmation on consequential actions, does not claim to be human, and does not replace administrator, accountant or bookkeeper expertise
